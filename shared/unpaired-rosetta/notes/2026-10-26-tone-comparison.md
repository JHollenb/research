# Tone comparison: paper.md draft 1 against the earlier notes and the authors' paper

Date: 2026-10-26

## Where paper.md read better

It is one thread, not two notes. The limitations sit in one place. Its abstract quotes the authors' own open problem.

## Where it read worse

The merge packed prose into dense paragraphs. Section 1 was a six-item list that repeated the abstract and then Sections 3 to 8. Many numbers appeared three times. Reproduction detail sat before the story. Several paragraphs held four or five ideas, such as the one on S1 as a selector over removal settings.

## Where it departed from the authors' language

The authors write "our aligner", "geometric initialization", "pseudo-pairs", "few-pair", "unpaired", "partially aligned" and "5 random seeds". They spell out FOSCTTM and centered kernel alignment with a plain gloss. They do not say "restarts". Draft 1 used "restarts", "pairs-free", "searchability", "nuisance direction" and "type test" with no early definition. They frame each experiment as a question with setup and results, and they state limits directly.

## Where the logic was hard to follow

The central question (does a rotation exist when the search fails?) appeared only in Section 3, after a long setup. Section 4.3 used S1 before Section 5 explained it. Section 7 repeated the CITE-seq numbers. Recommendation 6 comes from the first note and interrupts the thread.
