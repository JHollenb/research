# The Commit Layer Does Not Move: A Fixed Time Scaffold Hosts Per-Input Circuits

Jacob Hollenbeck

*Submission manuscript, 2026-10-02 (round-2 revision). Companion to [Time-Formed Circuits in Transformers](../time-formed-circuits/paper.md), which establishes the circuit class this paper reuses.*

## Abstract

In a trained model the layer where a factual answer commits does not move with the input. Across 42 fresh facts the commit lands at Gemma-2-2B layer 22 with standard deviation zero and at Qwen2.5-1.5B layer 23 (SD 1.16), and a preregistered retest on fresh colors (48 Qwen, 106 Gemma) holds the color commit input-invariant under all three frozen locators. We call the fixed schedule — seed, formation, commit, store, consumer — the time scaffold, and the per-input computation on it a space circuit: the operation re-weights a recurring reader, the fact selects which stored row is read. Byte-identical stored state acquires query-dependent authority (median 3.130 nats, 16/16). A reader profile frozen before evaluation predicts held relation contexts in all eight; permuting the value-projection rows collapses it. In a re-analysis the operation's weighting carries eleven times the fact-like variance and 84.0% of store necessity; this split is learned, collapsing to ~1% in random or shuffled nets — themselves incompetent (top-1 0/96), bounding the claim to learned, not scaffold-versus-any-competent-net. Of three preregistered falsifiers two held and the third, a write-window locator, fired in two of six earlier cells; the retest cleared the color cell under all three locators, while the argmax locator still trips on the identity plateau (Gemma 4.06, commit SD 0.00), as preregistered; the cross-family common graph remains a null. The same organization holds in a state-space and a diffusion model; standard attribution tools find the space circuit, not the scaffold.

## 1. Introduction

A circuit in a neural network is usually read as a wiring diagram: a set of components whose activations, interchanged or ablated, change the output. The companion paper to this one shows that for factual recall this picture is incomplete. The state that controls the answer is not present at the input and read off by a fixed component; it is *built* across the layers of a forward pass, committed to a late store, and only then consumed. We call that construction a time circuit.

This paper makes a stronger architectural claim about the same object. The time circuit is a scaffold. By scaffold we mean an internal, learned schedule of causal stages — not a prompt template or a chain-of-thought scaffold, which are external text structures. Each trained model has a fixed such schedule — seed, then formation over ordered execution, then a commit band, then a store, then a consumer — and the location of each stage is a property of the architecture and the training, not of the input. The space circuit, by contrast, is chosen per forward pass; we use "space" in the sense of the companion taxonomy, a locally decisive operation at a position, and gloss it here as the per-input read-and-weight computation on the scaffold.

**Thesis.** In a trained model the location where the controlling state commits and is read is fixed by the architecture and its training and does not move with the input (the time scaffold), while per input the operation re-weights a recurring set of reader coordinates and the fact selects which stored row is read (the space circuit).

This thesis is a unification as much as a discovery, and we state the mapping to prior work up front and claim only the delta. The fixed schedule of stages is the causal counterpart of the staged recall of Geva et al. and the universal "stages of inference" of Lad et al.; the operation re-weighting a recurring reader is close to a function vector in the sense of Todd et al. and Hendel et al., read at a late site; and the fact selecting a row is query-driven binding in the sense of Feng and Steinhardt. Our contribution is not these pieces but their joint, causal decomposition: per-fact causal input-invariance of the commit layer, a variance split of the read site into scaffold and operation terms, and the recurrence of this organization across a transformer, a state-space model, and a diffusion model.

Figure 1 states the division. The upper track is fixed across inputs; the lower track is instantiated anew for each input on that fixed track. The contribution of this paper is to measure the two tracks apart, with a preregistered test, and to show that the standard attribution tools of the field see only the lower track.

![Figure 1. A fixed time scaffold hosts per-input space circuits. The upper track — seed, formation, commit band, store, consumer — has a location set by the architecture and its training and does not move with the input. The lower track is the space circuit: for each forward pass the fact selects which stored row is read, the operation weights a recurring set of reader coordinates, and the query decides which stored row has causal authority.](figures/fig1_scaffold_schematic.png)

The claim matters for interpretation. If the scaffold is fixed and the space circuit is dynamic, then a tool that attributes the answer to input-specific features near the embedding will find the dynamic part and be blind to the scaffold where the answer is actually committed. We show this directly: on a factual-recall behavior in Gemma-2-2B, a leading attribution-graph method places almost all subject influence at the first layer and sees at most 0.10 to 0.21 of the effect in the commit band, while a native cut of the committed store flips the answer 83% of the time.

The paper is organized around one preregistered test and a ring of supporting evidence. Section 2 defines the scaffold, the space circuit, and the three grains at which the space circuit is dynamic. Sections 3 and 4 show, respectively, that the scaffold location coincides with architecture and that the space circuit is chosen per input. Section 5 is the central test: the preregistered, prospective measurement is the per-fact commit layer; the variance split of the read site is a retrospective re-analysis of earlier data and is reported as such. Of three preregistered falsifiers, two held and the third (the write-window locator) fired as frozen in two of six cells; a preregistered confirmatory retest on fresh colors has since held the color commit input-invariant under all three frozen locators (the argmax locator still trips on the identity plateau, as preregistered), so the invariance no longer rests on a post-hoc locator. Section 6 shows the scaffold lives in the trained weights. Section 7 finds a consistent organization in a state-space model and a diffusion model. Section 8 shows that formed state feeds later computation, within bounds. Section 9 is an honest null: the cross-family common-graph claim that an earlier version of this work led with does not survive a discriminating test, and the thesis does not need it. Section 10 gives the engineering converse — programs and state compiled once and installed at the fixed site run across new inputs through the native consumer, while matched wrong-site and wrong-program controls fail.

Every quantity is measured with the unchanged native consumer — full-vocabulary logits for language models, the native scheduler and decoder for diffusion — on stock Hugging Face eager execution in float32. Effective sample sizes are stated per claim, because the replication unit is the context, not the intervention. Where a claim rests on a single family or a single seed, the text says so.

## 2. Definitions

A time circuit is the construction, commitment, and consumption of the state that controls a model's answer, laid out along the model's ordered execution. For a transformer the ordering axis is layer depth; for a diffusion model it is the denoising step; for a state-space model it is the recurrent transition. This paper takes the existence and causal authority of that circuit as established by the companion work and asks what is fixed about it and what is free.

The **scaffold** is the fixed part. It is the schedule of stages — a seed, a formation window over ordered execution, a commit band, a store, and a consumer — together with the location of each stage in the model. The claim of this paper is that in a trained checkpoint these locations are properties of the architecture and the training: they are the same for different facts, different operations, and different sentence templates. The scaffold is not a constant tensor and it is not the engineered module graph; it is where, along execution, the controlling state forms and is read.

The **space circuit** is the free part. It is the response-specific computation instantiated on the scaffold for one forward pass: which stored row is read, with what payload, and how the reader is weighted. It is "space" in the sense of the companion taxonomy — a locally decisive operation at a position — but here it is explicitly hosted on the time scaffold rather than standing alone.

The space circuit is dynamic at three grains, which we keep separate because they carry different evidence.

1. **Row selection.** Which stored row the reader attends to and consumes. This is expected to move with the fact, because different facts live in different rows.
2. **Operation weighting.** How strongly each reader coordinate is weighted. This is expected to move with the operation — asking for identity versus a relation re-weights a recurring set of readers.
3. **Site rebuild.** Whether the set of read sites is genuinely different per input — a new local circuit each time. This is the grain the thesis predicts is *not* used: the reader is re-weighted and re-pointed, not rebuilt.

Several measurement terms recur. A **coordinate** is one read site in the late key/value interface, identified by (layer, key/value head, key-or-value); the 24-coordinate reader used below is six late layers by two stored heads by the key/value split. A **signed reader profile** is the per-coordinate causal effect of cutting that coordinate on the answer, a vector over coordinates. A **same-parent** intervention fixes a stored state and its intervention bytes exactly and changes only the surrounding native context, so that any behavioral difference is attributable to how the state is used rather than to a different state. **Query-selective harm** is the difference in a stored patch's effect on the answer when its fact is queried versus when the other fact is queried; a positive value means the identical bytes matter more when their fact is asked for. Throughout, a context is **admitted** only if the clean model answers it correctly with a margin above a fixed threshold; non-admitted contexts are retained in the records but excluded from the quantitative panels, and the admitted count is the effective n.

## 3. The scaffold is set by architecture

The scaffold's location is a property of the architecture, visible first in where a model commits its store. In Gemma-2-2B the commit lands at specific late layers: the four layers spanning L16–L22 carry 0.58 of the store's necessity together and at most 0.08 each, while the interleaved layers L17 and L21 write slightly negative (−0.03 to −0.08) and, cut alone, *raise* the clean answer by 0.18 (companion paper; n = 8–42 admitted items per cell, single seed; Gemma-2-2B). These committing layers coincide with Gemma-2's sliding-window layers, but that coincidence is not caused by attention type, and we tested it directly (Figure B, Appendix D). At our prompt lengths — 17 tokens, far below the 4096-token window — the sliding mask is numerically identical to the full causal mask, so flipping a layer's attention type between sliding and global is an exact no-op (maximum logit change 0.0 in both directions), while a genuine window-4 mask changes the logits by 19.6, confirming the test is live. Under the type swap the per-layer commit profile is byte-identical and still positive at L16/18/20/22 and non-positive at the global L17/L21. The commit therefore tracks layer index and depth, not attention type; the sliding/global alternation is confounded with parity at this context length. This does not weaken the thesis — the scaffold is fixed by depth — but it leaves the attention-type reading untested at long context, where sliding and global masks diverge; Gemma-3, whose 5:1 ratio would separate them, was not available (Gemma-2-2B, 42 identity items, fp32 eager).

The companion paper establishes the rest of the schedule, which we cite rather than re-derive: the commit sits at a recurring fractional depth (roughly 0.69 to 0.83 in Mamba, about 0.7 to 0.9 in transformers) and is a depth-fixed write port whose causal lever is the input write, not retention — the write is the larger lever in all six Mamba size-by-task cells (companion). The store forms over a window that closes near the commit — a single residual write of the clean subject state authors the answer from an earlier layer and then falls off, sharply in Qwen2.5-0.5B and Mamba-130M and more gradually in Pythia-410M — and its necessity is distributed across a late band rather than one layer (in Qwen2.5-1.5B the best single layer carries 0.08 against 0.96 for the six-layer band L22–L27), which is why a single-component tool underreads it (companion paper; single seed).

What this paper adds is that the commit location does not move when the input changes, measured fact by fact. Figure 2 shows the commit layer per fresh fact, located by the closing edge of the formation window. In Gemma-2-2B every one of 42 identity facts commits at layer 22, a standard deviation of exactly 0.00; in Qwen2.5-1.5B the 42 identity facts commit at a median layer 23 with SD 1.16. The band location is a constant of the checkpoint, not a per-input choice (stock HF eager, float32, single seed; 42 admitted identity facts each). One caveat, developed in Section 5: this closing-edge locator is post hoc; the per-fact locator we preregistered was the argmax of the write curve, which proved ill-chosen and fired the write-window falsifier. A preregistered confirmatory retest on fresh colors (48 facts in Qwen, 106 in Gemma) has since held the color commit input-invariant under all three frozen locators (the argmax locator still trips on the Gemma identity plateau, as preregistered), so the invariance does not rest on the post-hoc locator alone.

![Figure 2. The commit layer is input-invariant. Per fresh fact, measured by the closing edge of the formation window (a post-hoc commit locator; see Section 5), Gemma-2-2B commits at layer 22 (SD 0.00 across 42 identity facts) and Qwen2.5-1.5B at layer 23 (SD 1.16). Across operations the commit shifts by 0 layers in Qwen2.5-1.5B and 2 in Gemma-2-2B. The scaffold location is a constant of the checkpoint. (Decisive-test panel F1.)](figures/fig2_band_location.png)

Across operations the commit barely moves either. The peak-necessity layer shifts by 0 layers between identity and color in Qwen2.5-1.5B, 1 in Qwen2.5-0.5B, and 2 in Gemma-2-2B, and the layer main effect carries 66% to 98% of the per-layer necessity curve's variance. Whatever the model is asked, it commits in nearly the same place (single seed; three transformer checkpoints).

## 4. The space circuit is chosen per input

The lead result that the space circuit is dynamic is a same-parent contrast: the same stored bytes acquire different causal authority under different questions. In Qwen2.5-1.5B we fix a late key/value store and its intervention bytes exactly — same donor state, same layer band, same positions, same tensor digests — and change only the native question. Figure 3 shows the query-selective harm. Asking for the patched fact rather than the other fact increases the patch's harm to the correct answer by a median 3.130 nats for color (range 2.066 to 5.565, positive in all 16 of 16 contrasts), 0.583 nats for position (0.424 to 1.302, 16/16), and 3.021 nats for a position-to-color lookup (2.236 to 4.772, 16/16). The identical stored state is consumed differently because the current query changes its use (16 same-parent contrasts per operation across four scenes, 128 directional pairing checks; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

![Figure 3. The lead result: a byte-identical stored patch has query-dependent authority. Fixing the stored key/value state and its bytes exactly and changing only the native question, the patch's harm to the correct answer is larger when its own fact is queried — by a median 3.130 nats for color, 0.583 for position, and 3.021 for a position-to-color lookup, positive in all 16 of 16 contrasts each. The space circuit that consumes the store is chosen per input.](figures/fig3_same_patch_query.png)

The store also hosts several operations off one fixed interface. Replacing the queried late fact span with a coherent donor state redirects the native answer to the donor's color in 16/16 contexts and the donor's position in 12/16, and identity can additionally be redirected at the subject anchor, with the donor animal appearing in all 10 available continuations. One prior-fixed interface carries color, position, and identity reads; it does not follow that each read uses an identical microscopic circuit (single seed; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

The operation's role is to re-weight a recurring reader, not to rebuild it. Figure 4 shows the signed causal reader profile — the per-coordinate effect over the late key/value interface. Within one operation the profile recurs across different queried facts with cosine 0.947 for identity, 0.927 for color, and 0.961 for relation, and the same two value routes carry every operation. Averaging out single-measurement noise across fresh facts, the within-operation reader is essentially identical (cosine 0.92 to 0.999 in both Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B). The reader is a fixed object that operations tune (Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B). This recurring-reader-reused-across-inputs picture is the per-coordinate, causal form of the task-general component reuse of [Merullo et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08744): where they show a small set of components recurs across tasks, our signed per-coordinate variance split says the reader is re-weighted, not rebuilt.

![Figure 4. The operation re-weights a recurring reader; the fact does not rebuild it. Within one operation the signed reader profile recurs across facts with cosine 0.93 to 0.96 (0.92 to 0.999 after denoising fresh facts). Across operations it diverges — identity and relation share only cosine 0.336 — because identity loads one value route and relation the other. The reader is tuned by the operation, not rebuilt by the fact.](figures/fig4_reader_cosine.png)

Across operations the same reader diverges, because the operation selects which route to load. Identity and relation share only cosine 0.336 of their reader profile; identity loads the L23 key/value-head-0 value route with weight −1.18, relation loads the L22 key/value-head-1 route with −0.30, and color loads both. Operation weighting is the dominant dynamic grain (grain 2 of Section 2); identity-to-relation divergence is the cleanest case because the two operations load different routes (Qwen2.5-1.5B). This operation weighting is, in form, a function vector read at a late site, so the sharpest test of whether the "space circuit" is more than function vectors under a new name is whether a Todd-style operation vector already reproduces the identity-versus-relation route shift. That equivalence test has now run, and the answer is no (Figure C). Following Todd et al., we formed an operation vector as the mean difference of the output-projection activation between the relational and identity operations and injected it at layer 24. It produced no top-1 flip (0 of 14), moving the relational target by +2.19 nats and the color target by only +0.16, and it did not rebuild the reader: it erased the identity value route (the L23 key/value-head-0 contribution went from −0.808 to +0.007) without constructing the relational one. The per-input operation weighting re-points a recurring reader in a way an additive steering vector does not reproduce — consistent with the constructive result of Section 10 that what sits at the site is a program, not a direction. This is a bounded negative: the vector summed over all heads rather than the top causal heads, its coefficient was fixed at one, it was extracted from all contexts including the held ones, and the injection layer (24) sat at the top of the searched grid; a top-head, scale-swept follow-up has not been run.

![Figure C. An additive operation function vector does not reproduce the operation weighting (reviewer control). A Todd-style relation-minus-identity output-projection vector injected at layer 24 gives no top-1 flip (0 of 14) and moves the relational target by only +2.19 nats; it erases the identity value route (L23.KV0 −0.808 → +0.007) without building the relational route. The operation re-weights a recurring reader, which this additive vector does not capture.](figures/figC_fv_reproduction.png)

What moves with the fact is which row is read, not the reader. The raw attention mass over the target span across two facts has cosine 0.63, 0.61, and 0.70 for identity, color, and relation — markedly less stable than the intervention-effect reader profile, which holds at 0.95, 0.93, and 0.96. The selected rows move while the reader skeleton holds, which is exactly row selection (grain 1) over a fixed reader, not site rebuild (grain 3) (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

## 5. Separating the two tracks: a per-fact commit test and a variance split

The two tracks can be measured apart on one fixed model, but the two measurements have different provenance and we keep them separate. One part is preregistered and prospective: before collecting any new data we froze the design, the metrics, and three falsifiers for a per-fact run that measures the commit band location on fresh facts. The other part — the variance split of the read-site map into scaffold and operation terms — is a retrospective re-analysis of causal site maps collected earlier, on an operation set that includes a distinct relational operation. We do not claim the split itself was preregistered. The specimens are Qwen2.5-1.5B (28 layers, 2 key/value heads) and Gemma-2-2B (26 layers, 4 key/value heads), on stock Hugging Face eager execution, float32, tf32 off, seed 0; every prospective cell passed its no-op and cache-parity gates and reproduced the prior commit-layer and read-map canaries exactly.

The read-site variance decomposition (Figure 5, left) is that retrospective re-analysis. Over a fully crossed design in Qwen2.5-1.5B — 24 coordinates × 3 operations × 2 lexical sets × 2 templates × 2 word orders × 2 queried bindings, 1,152 cells — the signed reader-profile variance attributes 29.0% to the coordinate-by-operation interaction (operation weighting) and 2.7% to fact-like terms (coordinate-by-fact 1.1% plus coordinate-by-binding 1.6%), a ratio of about eleven to one for operation over fact. We lead with that ratio rather than with the 42.2% coordinate main effect, because a large main effect — different coordinates simply carrying different average causal magnitude — could in principle be expected in any structured network, trained or not. That control has now run, on an untrained and on a per-layer weight-shuffled Qwen2.5-1.5B of identical shape (Figure A, Appendix D), and it comes out in favor of the scaffold: the split is learned. A canary reproduces the published split (42.2/29.0/1.1), but in both nulls the coordinate main effect collapses to 1.2–1.4% and the operation-by-fact ratio falls from 19.0 in the trained network to about one (0.62 shuffled, 1.10 random), while permuting only the L22/L23 value projections keeps most of it (ratio 11.9). (This 19.0 is coordinate-by-operation over coordinate-by-fact on the four-lexical-set null design; it is a different cut from the eleven-to-one headline ratio above, which pools the fact-like binding term in with the fact variance.) The reviewer's hypothesis that the coordinate main effect is a trivial property of any structured network failed, and the corresponding falsifier fired: the 42.2% is a property of the trained weights. The preregistered interaction predictions held — trained coordinate-by-operation exceeds both nulls by 17-fold and 7.8-fold, and the trained ratio exceeds five while the nulls do not — and the relational reader beats the operation-blind profile in the trained network (cosine 0.997 versus 0.485) but, we report honestly rather than explain away, also in the random-initialized network (a positive margin of 0.350), a failed prediction. One bound is essential: both nulls are behaviorally incompetent (native top-1 0 of 96), so this control shows the read-site structure is learned, not that it is a scaffold rather than whatever any competent network would learn. Two disclosures: the ratio interval is a leave-one-lexical-set-out jackknife (16.3 to 30.6) where the preregistration promised a bootstrap, and although the predictions were fixed in a timestamped preregistration carried as a design hash in every job, the frozen manifest was re-written after the results were read. The fact factor here has four lexical sets (coordinate-by-fact 1.6%); the prospective per-fact band location below carries the fact-invariance claim directly.

Store necessity at the committed band decomposes the same way (Figure 5, middle), on the same kind of earlier site maps: replacing the whole late band over a crossed operation × scene × order × binding design (64 cells) assigns 84.0% of the effect variance to the operation and 3.2% to the fact, with 2.0% to the operation-by-fact interaction and a 10.4% residual. How much store is needed is set overwhelmingly by the operation (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

The commit band location, measured per fresh fact by the close of the write window, is the tightest term of all — with the caveat, developed in the next paragraph, that this commit locator is post hoc; the locator we preregistered was ill-chosen. Under the commit locator, Gemma-2-2B identity commits at layer 22 with SD 0.00 across 42 facts; Qwen2.5-1.5B identity at median layer 23 with SD 1.16; Gemma-2-2B color at layer 20 with SD 1.11 across 25 facts. Across operations the median commit shifts by 0 layers in Qwen2.5-1.5B and 2 in Gemma-2-2B. The whole band is necessary — an isolated band swap changes the answer margin by more than half a nat — for 100% of admitted facts in every cell.

We preregistered three falsifiers; two held and the third fired, and we report the firing plainly. The band-location falsifier — the per-fact peak-necessity layer moving more than four layers across facts — held. The reader-rebuild falsifier — the per-fact reader varying as much as the per-operation reader — held on the re-analysis (coordinate-by-fact 1.1% against coordinate-by-operation 29.0%; denoised per-fact reader cosine 0.92 to 0.999). The third falsifier, on the write-formation window, fired. Its frozen statistic was the standard deviation, across facts within a cell, of the per-item argmax layer of the residual-write curve, with a four-layer threshold, and that frozen statistic exceeded four in two of six cells: Qwen2.5-1.5B color at 5.45 and Gemma-2-2B identity at 4.01 (Figure 5, right, shows the commit-locator spreads, not this frozen argmax statistic). The frozen locator was ill-chosen. The write-gain curve is a near-flat early plateau that steps down at the commit, so its argmax lands on the noisy plateau — median argmax layer 0 for Qwen color and 13 for Gemma identity — rather than at the commit. Under a post-hoc commit locator, the last layer still at half-maximum gain, the window closes at an input-invariant layer in five of six cells (Gemma identity SD 0.00 across 42 facts, Qwen identity 1.16, Gemma color 1.11, Qwen shape 0.75, Gemma shape 1.88), with Qwen color the exception at 4.17; every per-fact SD quoted elsewhere in this paper uses this commit locator. The invariance claim therefore no longer rests on a post-hoc locator. We ran a preregistered confirmatory retest on fresh colors — disjoint from the earlier color set — with all three locators (argmax S1, commit S2, necessity-argmin S3) and the pass rule (band-location stable iff S2 ≤ 4 and S3 ≤ 4 on write-signal-admitted facts) frozen in advance, reported whatever the outcome. In Qwen2.5-1.5B the 48 admitted fresh colors give S1 2.84, S2 1.44, S3 1.76; in Gemma-2-2B the 106 admitted fresh colors give S1 3.44, S2 0.90, S3 0.97. No fresh-color cell fires on any statistic, so the color band location is input-invariant under all three preregistered locators in both families. The Phase-2 Qwen-color firing does not reproduce: even the frozen argmax S1 falls below four on fresh, well-signaled colors. Three honesty items attach. The Qwen set covers only 27 unique colors, short of the frozen ≥40 count — the single-token filter dropped 14 colors and 36 presented items failed read-back — so we report it as a deviation; Gemma meets the count (54 colors). The Gemma run needed a post-freeze subject-chunking change to fit the card, numerics-neutral to about 2e-5 nats and identical on every compared item (custody PASS), and the freeze is evidenced by file modification time only, with no recorded hash. And the Gemma identity canary's frozen argmax S1 still fires at 4.06 while its commit SD is 0.00 — the argmax-of-early-plateau artifact the preregistration anticipated, not band-location instability.

One confound in the prospective run itself must be stated. Its three operations — identity, color, and shape — are all surface read-back, so within that run the read side cannot separate operation from fact; the clean operation-versus-fact separation (coordinate-by-operation 29.0% versus coordinate-by-fact 1.1%, and the identity-to-relation reader cosine of 0.336) comes from the earlier panels that include a distinct relational operation. We rely on the prospective run only for the per-fact band location, and on the earlier panels for the read-map split — which is why the split is labeled retrospective above. A preregistered addendum then put a relational operation (capital-of, whose answer is a different token from the swapped subject) into the fresh-fact run, closing the operation-versus-fact separation prospectively.

That addendum has now run (20 countries across two templates, 40 facts, capital admitted 40/40 in both models; prereg frozen before the run, by file timestamp), and it separates operation from fact cleanly (Table 1). Within an operation the per-item reader profile recurs across fresh facts at cosine 0.74 to 0.99. Across operations the relational reader is distinct: identity and capital share only cosine 0.204 in Qwen2.5-1.5B and 0.218 in Gemma-2-2B, roughly 0.6 below the within-operation values; by contrast identity and color agree at 0.842 and 0.911, at or above the within-operation per-item cosines, so the two surface read-back operations share one reader while the relational operation uses a distinct one. Of the two frozen second-falsifier statistics, the robust within-versus-across cosine test was not triggered in either model — the reader is re-weighted, not rebuilt — while the noise-sensitive sum-of-squares statistic fired for Gemma (coordinate-by-fact 0.58 against coordinate-by-operation 0.42) and not for Qwen (0.27 against 0.73); we report it as frozen, noting that it charges single-measurement noise to the fact term, which the denoised operation-mean cosines (0.976 to 0.999) show to be noise. The relational commit is input-invariant on the same commit locator used throughout — capital commits at layer 22 with SD 0.00 across 40 fresh facts in both models — while under the frozen argmax locator its spread is 5.39 in Qwen (above the four-layer threshold) and 2.14 in Gemma, again because the argmax locator tracks the write-gain plateau rather than the commit.

One apparent tension with prior work should be resolved here. [Agarwal 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17537) reports a temporal asymmetry in which relation information becomes generation-controlling before entity information; our addendum instead finds capital-of committing at the same layer as identity (layer 22, SD 0.00 in both models). The two are not in conflict because they measure different things: Agarwal compares when a relation type versus an entity token becomes available, read at the entity position, whereas we locate the layer at which the operation's controlling state commits at the final query position. A fixed commit layer for both operations is consistent with the relation being resolved earlier along the way; our claim is about where the controlling state is committed for readout, not about the order in which its ingredients become available.

## 6. The scaffold lives in the trained weights

If the scaffold is set by training, a profile of the reader frozen before evaluation should predict causal effects in contexts never opened, and disordering the trained weights that carry the reader should destroy it — in the spirit of the weight-level localization of memorized content by [Stoehr et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19851). Both hold. We froze a 24-coordinate signed reader profile over the late band, together with three competitors — an operation-blind mean profile, a wrong-operation profile, and a within-band coordinate permutation — before opening any held context, then evaluated on two lexical sets × two templates × two word orders. Figure 6 shows the result.

![Figure 6. The scaffold lives in the weights. A 24-coordinate reader profile frozen before evaluation predicts held relation contexts, beating three competitors (operation-blind, wrong-operation, coordinate-permutation) in all eight by raw error — mapped cosine 0.954 versus 0.566 operation-blind, raw RMSE 0.029 versus 0.149. Permuting all 256 rows of the L22/L23 value projections collapses the mapped cosine from 0.955 to 0.049 for relation, 0.838 to 0.110 for color, and 0.931 to 0.569 for identity. The measured profile depends on the trained value-projection row correspondence.](figures/fig6_confirmation_results.png)

The frozen profile predicts held relation contexts. For the relational operation the mapped profile beats every frozen competitor in raw error in all eight contexts — mapped cosine 0.954 against the operation-blind 0.566, mapped raw RMSE 0.029 against 0.149 — and its signed shape wins seven of eight contexts in the all-row view and all eight in the competence-conditioned view. The eight contexts are two lexical sets crossed with two templates and two orders, which are correlated replications rather than eight independent worlds; the prediction is prospective but its effective breadth is modest (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

The prediction is operation-specific, not uniform. Color retains the broad profile shape but misses its coarse reader ordering, winning only five of eight contexts, and the operation-blind profile has slightly lower median error there. Identity is readout-limited: most native identity continuations use a format the fixed readout does not score, and in the four contexts with a competent identity continuation the operation-blind comparator is better (0 of 4). Size never produces a native answer in the frozen format and is unavailable. The scaffold's reader transfers prospectively for the relational operation and partially for color; it is not a single uniform profile across operations (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

Disordering the trained value projections collapses the profile. Mild permutations of 5% and 20% of the rows of the L22 and L23 value projections leave the signed profile largely intact, but permuting all 256 rows of both projections, with their biases, changes the mapped cosine from 0.955 to 0.049 for relation, 0.838 to 0.110 for color, and 0.931 to 0.569 for identity. Native competence falls under the full permutation as well, so this is a dependence result, not a behavior-matched null: the measured reader depends on the final-checkpoint row correspondence of two specific value projections. It does not date when that organization was acquired (four opened contexts, post-hoc; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

## 7. A consistent organization in a state-space model and a diffusion model

The scaffold is a functional organization, not a tensor, so if it is general it should recur in architectures with different state interfaces. Two other families show the same organization at smaller scale, on single seeds; because the cross-family common graph is itself a null (Section 9), we report these as consistent with the division, not identical to it. Table 2 summarizes the three families.

| Family | Fixed scaffold (location) | Space circuit (per input) | Effective n |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transformer (Qwen2.5-1.5B, Gemma-2-2B) | commit band at a fixed late layer (SD 0.00–1.16/fact) | operation re-weights reader; query re-points row | 42 facts/model |
| State-space (Mamba-130M) | one late recurrent store at ~0.83 depth | same store serves identity and capital; reader re-weighted by operation | 12 rows; 8+4 held |
| Diffusion (FLUX/SDXL) | static prompt key/value bus; timing-indexed writes | live latent query; per-prompt program; early vs late write | 4 cases; 21/21 replay |

*Table 2. The scaffold / space-circuit division across three model families, at single-seed scale (consistent with the division, not identical; the cross-family common graph is the null of Section 9).*

In Mamba-130M one shared recurrent state serves two operations: reading a country's identity and reading its capital are both carried by the same late state. Delivering only the successor state's content changes the later answer in all 12 qualified rows, and a frozen 27-coordinate profile predicts two held country pairs — capital in all eight rows (mapped cosine 0.9941 against the operation-blind 0.9880) and delayed identity in all four (0.9586 against 0.9102). One recurrent store, read two ways with the reader re-weighted by operation, is consistent with the transformer division (single seed; Mamba-130M).

The Mamba commit is likewise a depth-fixed write port, as Section 3 reported: the write dose is the causal lever in all six size-by-task cells, and retention is a correlate (companion paper). The shared state's reads are operation-dependent, and the clock-dose and reader effects change with the operation and the token boundary, which is the space circuit varying on a fixed carried-state scaffold (companion paper; single seed; Mamba-130M/370M/2.8B).

Diffusion shows the scaffold as a fixed bus carrying a per-prompt program. In text-to-image models the prompt compiles into a static key/value program and the live latent issues a dynamic query; installing the static source program reproduces the native pixel output byte-for-byte in 21 of 21 cases across seven specimens and five diffusion families, including prompts never used to build the tooling, while wrong-source and wrong-sign controls diverge by 57 to 97 in mean absolute pixel error (0–255 RGB scale). The machinery is prompt-agnostic; the program it carries is not — four new scene families reused the same physical route but ran different logical topologies (7 specimens, 5 families).

The timing of a write on the diffusion scaffold decides which kind of edit it produces, which is the diffusion analogue of the formation window. Installing the same source state early in denoising changes composition and framing; installing it late changes iris color while preserving composition. A four-case reproduction reached violet eyes with a late write and left them blue with an early write, reproducing 12 native references exactly across 56 images. Where on the schedule the space circuit acts determines what it can change (single model, four matched cases).

## 8. Formed state feeds later computation (bounded)

State formed on the scaffold can feed a later step, though the evidence here is thin and single-seed and we present it as a bounded existence proof, not a frequency claim. In Qwen2.5-1.5B, in a development organism and in one of three held city/country contexts, replacing only a second formed row — with the first formed state, the visible intermediate token, the prefix, the later query, and the recipient's final hidden state all fixed — changes the later answer from France to Germany; zeroing it breaks the answer to a function word, and reinstating it restores France. The other two held contexts are causal-but-incompetent or fail to form the target, so the competent held n is 1 of 3. A separate test shows a development-frozen payload store changes the native authorship of its entity in 7 of 8 held reference operations while preserving the other entity in all 8 (two values × two orders; Qwen2.5-1.5B). The companion paper carries the quantitative mediation that grounds this — blocking the recipient-formed late band removes a median 0.924 of an upstream seed's answer-gain, and transplanting it into an unseeded recipient recovers 0.871 across 42 rows — and establishes the store as a mediator; our only addition is that the formed store is then read by a per-input space circuit.

## 9. What does not generalize

An earlier version of this work led with a cross-family common role graph — a single scaffold shared across transformers, state-space models, and diffusion models. That claim does not survive a discriminating test, and we report it as a null. The thesis of this paper — a fixed scaffold per model, with per-input space circuits on it — does not need a cross-family common graph, and is not weakened by its failure. Figure 7 shows the two decisive nulls.

![Figure 7. The cross-family common-graph claim is a null. Left: a connected scaffold forecast ties a plain additive baseline exactly (MAE 0.015099 versus 0.015099) and is slightly worse than operation-blind and query-only controls (0.014593, 0.014588), winning only 1 of 3 contexts. Right: the best common role-matcher costs 6.9125 against a depth null's 6.9511, a gap of 0.0386. On these tests a shared cross-family graph cannot be told apart from addition or from matched depth.](figures/fig7_honest_null.png)

A connected cross-family forecast ties plain addition exactly. Predicting the held connected effect from the scaffold gives a mean absolute error of 0.015099 logit units, against 0.014593 for an operation-blind constant and 0.014588 for a query-only account, and it ties a four-feature additive baseline at 0.015099 to the digit. It wins only 1 of 3 held contexts against each control. On this test the scaffold forecast cannot be distinguished from addition — which is the expected outcome if factual recall is itself an additive composition of independent mechanisms, as Chughtai et al. argue ([Chughtai et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07321)) — so we claim no cross-family graph beyond addition (3 held contexts; Qwen2.5-1.5B route to a recipient successor).

A common role-matcher is indistinguishable from a depth null. The best alignment of roles across families costs 6.9125 against the next assignment's 6.9511 and a depth null nearly matched, a gap of 0.0386. It uses opened outcomes and differently resolved assays and cannot establish a unique cross-family alignment.

Two transfers also miss. The color reader ordering predicted from the profile misses in all eight held contexts, even though color's full 24-coordinate profile remains partially predictive. And the prospective cross-family core that does align — the single edge from formed carried state to the unchanged native consumer, where the mean profiles of the two families match at RMSE 0.0928 against 0.8526 under a permuted-role map — rests on an effective sample of two diffusion rows, two state-space rows, and one competent transformer row. That is a suggestive common edge, not a shared graph, and we state it as such.

The honest reading is that the within-model scaffold is strong and prospectively confirmed, while the across-model common graph is not. The paper's claims are the former.

## 10. Constructive evidence: the fixed site is addressable

The thesis has an engineering converse that is an independent check. If the commit-and-read location is fixed and shared across inputs while only the space circuit is chosen per input, then the per-input part should be extractable — compiled once against one input and installed at the fixed location into a different input, where the model's own unchanged consumer runs it. If the location instead moved per input, no such transplant could drive a new input through its native consumer. We built both a diffusion and a language/state-space version, read every result from the native consumer under the paper's admission and no-op gates, and the converse holds.

In text-to-image models the prompt compiles into a static source program read at the cross-attention sites while the live latent issues the query. Capturing that program from one prompt and installing it into a neutral recipient reproduces the recorded native pixels byte-for-byte in 21 of 21 cases across seven specimens and five diffusion families, at mean absolute pixel error 0.0 with no new model load, including prompts never used to build the tooling. The controls decide it: the wrong prompt's program, or the correct program with its sign reversed, installed at the same sites, diverges by 57 to 97 in mean absolute pixel error (0–255 RGB scale) against 0.0 for the correct one (n = 3 prompts). The site is prompt-agnostic; only the matching program on it reproduces the image.

What is installed is a program, not a steering vector. On a Boolean-function transplant into fresh language-model processes, a compiled XOR program produces the correct completion in 148 of 150 spans against 0 of 150 for native generation, and a hostile inverted (XNOR) program installed on the same machine executes the opposite semantics in 148 of 150 rows. An additive steering vector would not invert with the program. This sharpens Section 4: the operation weighting is function-vector-like, but the object sitting at the fixed site is executable content the consumer runs, not a direction added to the output.

In a transformer the same decomposition is constructive once the committed store is externalized as addressable pages. Writing a correctly formed state into the fixed formation site authors the intended answer (median normalized effect 1.011 across 14 held facts), while the identical residual write at the wrong site does essentially nothing (−0.133) and a shuffled payload at the right site is destroyed (−0.607); a key/value write at the site recovers 0.602 against −0.153 at the wrong site. We keep two weaknesses of this panel visible: native competence is limited (clean top-1 on 6 of 14 rows), and the read-cut role is less specific than the write. A parallel result in preparation extends this to a reasoning step: an injected state-only key/value swap at the fixed site changes the answer while the visible intermediate step token is left unchanged, so the controlling content sits in the state at the site, not in the emitted token.

![Figure 8. Constructive evidence that the site is fixed and addressable. A prompt's source program, captured and installed into a neutral recipient at the fixed cross-attention sites, reproduces the native render byte-for-byte (shown); the companion control sheet (`figures/fig9_qkv_controls_proof.png`) shows the wrong-program, wrong-sign, and shuffled-row arms diverging at the same sites. The machine is fixed; only the matching program on it reproduces the image.](figures/fig8_scene_compiler_proof.png)

These systems are a working proof of the fixed-machine, swappable-program decomposition, not of a universal scaffold. The results are mechanics-level and small-model, on single seeds, not a production service; the efficiency figures of the virtual machine come from separate experiments and are not additive. The compiled programs are derived from a donor input and installed at host-supplied addresses, so there is no donor-free compiler and no learned address resolver, and language-to-address selection and multi-hop reads are not established. The diffusion reproduction is a parity result that reproduces the native model's mistakes as faithfully as its successes, on a small, correlated prompt bank. Crucially, these constructions show the site is fixed and shared across inputs; they do not by themselves show it is learned — that claim rests on the weight-permutation result of Section 6 — and the cross-family common graph remains the null of Section 9.

## 11. Related work

The nearest predecessor to the scaffold is the staged, depth-structured view of inference. Lad et al. infer four universal stages at fixed relative depths across eight models from layer deletion, swapping, and logit alignment, and Geva et al. separate subject enrichment, relation propagation, and attention-mediated extraction into stages ([Lad et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19384); [Geva et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14767)); Nanda et al.'s fact-finding sequence gives a concatenate-lookup-extract account of recall ([Nanda et al. 2023](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/iGuwZTHWb6DFY3sKB/fact-finding-attempting-to-reverse-engineer-factual-recall)), and iterative-inference lenses read the by-layer refinement directly ([Belrose et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08112)). Our delta over all of these is a per-fact causal invariance metric: the commit layer, located fact by fact, has standard deviation 0.00 across 42 Gemma facts under the commit locator (and stays input-invariant under all three preregistered locators on the fresh-color retest), where prior work infers stages from aggregate robustness or logit trajectories rather than per-input causal location.

Factual recall also has a well-developed localization and editing literature. Meng et al. locate factual associations in mid-layer MLPs and edit them, and Hase et al. show that localization and editing can come apart ([Meng et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262); [Hase et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213)). The closest contemporaneous work on when commitment happens by depth is [Agarwal 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17537), who reports a temporal asymmetry in which relation information becomes generation-controlling before entity information across four decoder models and eight prompt families. Our delta over Agarwal is a per-fact causal input-invariance of the commit layer — SD 0.00 across 42 Gemma facts under the commit locator — where Agarwal establishes a cross-type ordering rather than per-fact invariance; the apparent tension with our finding that capital-of commits at the same layer as identity is resolved in Section 5 (the two measure different quantities at different positions). Our contribution is otherwise orthogonal to where a fact lives: we show that the location at which the controlling state commits is input-invariant, while the reader that consumes it is re-weighted per operation and re-pointed per fact, giving that deferral a fixed schedule.

Context-dependent binding and reusable operation carriers are the closest predecessors to the space-circuit side of our account. Feng and Steinhardt identify binding-identity representations through causal interventions; Todd et al. and Hendel et al. show that a reusable operation can be carried as a compact vector in model state; and Merullo et al. show that a small set of task-general circuit components is reused across tasks ([Feng and Steinhardt 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191); [Todd et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15213); [Hendel et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15916); [Merullo et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08744)). Our same-parent assay adds a different question — how identical stored bytes acquire different authority under a changed query — and our variance decomposition sharpens the reuse claim into a signed per-coordinate split that shows the reader is re-weighted rather than rebuilt. We do not claim every dynamic circuit is a compact vector.

Attribution-graph and automated-circuit methods are the tools our scaffold is partly invisible to. Automated circuit discovery and prompt-local attribution graphs with interpretable replacement models recover input-specific feature graphs, and attention-level tracing decomposes reads through query-key feature interactions ([Conmy et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997); [Ameisen et al. 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html); [Kamath et al. 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attention-qk/index.html); [Lindsey et al. 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html)). The companion paper shows directly that one such method, on a recall behavior in Gemma-2-2B, places most subject influence at the first layer and sees at most 0.10 to 0.21 of the effect in the commit band while a native store cut flips the answer 83% of the time, with about 30% of influence running through error nodes. These tools find the per-input space circuit; the scaffold is in the state they treat as an endpoint. Geiger et al.'s causal-abstraction framework supplies the standard against which our role graph is a candidate abstraction with incomplete alignment ([Geiger et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997)).

Diffusion and state-space interpretability supply the cross-architecture tests, and both have direct predecessors we build on. Sharma et al. port causal tracing and ROME to Mamba and localize mid-to-late causal components, which precedes our Mamba commit-depth and shared-state results ([Sharma et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03646)); Basu et al. localize diffusion knowledge with causal mediation and find it distributed across the UNet with a causal text-encoder state, which precedes our static-bus reading ([Basu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13730)). Cross-attention editing and transcoder-based diffusion tracing establish that prompt binding and temporal organization are addressable, and the Pythia and Mamba families supply developmental and recurrent specimens ([Hertz et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01626); [Mazur et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15796); [Biderman et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01373); [Gu and Dao 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)). Self-repair and the Hydra effect explain why a single removed component is often repaired downstream, which is why we measure whole bands rather than single layers ([McGrath et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15771)). Whether the scaffold is shared across seeds, in the sense of the universality studied by Gurnee et al., is the within-model-versus-universal question our Section 9 null leaves open ([Gurnee et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12181)).

## 12. Limitations

The scope is two transformer checkpoints, one small state-space model, and one diffusion family, at a single seed under a prototype-first policy. The variance decomposition and the band-location result are measured on Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B; the state-space results are Mamba-130M with sweeps to 2.8B; the diffusion results are FLUX and SDXL. This bounds a stable scaffold with per-input space circuits in these models; it is not a universal-architecture proof, and the cross-family common graph of Section 9 is an explicit null.

Effective sample sizes are small and correlated, and the replication unit is the context, not the intervention. The prospective relation prediction rests on eight correlated contexts from two lexical sets; the formed-state feedback result is competent in 1 of 3 held contexts; the cross-family common edge is two plus two plus one rows. The per-fact band-location result is the best-powered claim, at 42 admitted identity facts per model. Where a number is a single-measurement cosine we denoise by averaging within a template and say so; the raw per-item read-map cosines of 0.72 to 0.90 are noise, not a weaker signal.

One instrument defect is worth stating precisely, because it bears on how much of this work to trust. An earlier feedback instrument installed a custom attention function without its matching mask, so a multi-token query chunk could attend to future tokens — a correctness failure that nonetheless passed the instrument's own replay checks. That defect was confined to a retired selector-and-feedback instrument that this paper does not rely on. It does not touch the results here: the variance decomposition, the same-parent query contrast, the frozen-profile prediction, the formation-window and commit-band sweeps, and the France-to-Germany feedback all run on stock Hugging Face eager execution, and a corrected re-run of the affected routing measurement reproduced its qualitative effect under untouched eager execution with zero future attention in every preceding query row. The lesson we take is that a verification apparatus certifies that an instrument ran, not that its result is valid, and that the headline claims of this paper should be re-derived by independent groups on stock execution.

Several mechanistic questions remain open. We localize the store to a band and the reader to a set of coordinates, but not to a minimal set of heads or to the attention-versus-MLP split of the writer. We show the query re-points the reader but do not trace the query-to-row addressing algorithm, and a conventional learned lookup remains a compatible implementation. And the weight-permutation result ties the reader to the final checkpoint without dating when the organization was acquired; a same-assay checkpoint trajectory is the direct test and is not yet run.

## 13. Conclusion

The time circuit that controls a model's factual answer is a fixed scaffold. In a trained checkpoint the store forms and is read at a location that does not move when the fact, the operation, or the template changes: the commit layer is input-invariant per fresh fact, and a preregistered retest on fresh colors confirms the color commit under all three locators (the argmax locator still trips on the identity plateau, as preregistered), after the write-window falsifier had fired as frozen in two of six earlier cells. On a re-analysis of the read site, the operation's weighting dominates both the reader and store necessity, and that split is learned — it collapses in untrained and shuffled nets, bounded only by those nets' behavioral incompetence.

The space circuit is chosen per input on that scaffold: identical stored bytes carry different authority under different questions, the operation re-weights a recurring reader rather than rebuilding it, and the fact moves which row is read. A reader profile frozen before evaluation predicts held relation contexts, and permuting the trained value-projection rows collapses it, so the scaffold lives in the weights. The same organization — one store, read several ways, with a depth-fixed commit — holds at single-seed scale in a state-space model and a diffusion model's prompt-agnostic bus.

The consequence for interpretation is concrete. Standard attribution tools find the input-specific features near the embedding, which is the space circuit, and are nearly blind to the committed store, which is the scaffold. A circuit account that stops at the per-input graph describes the part of the computation the input chooses and misses the part the architecture fixes. The honest boundary is that this holds within a model: the cross-family common graph that would make the scaffold universal ties a plain additive baseline and is reported as a null.

To close with the thesis as stated at the outset: in a trained model the location where the controlling state commits and is read is fixed by the architecture and its training and does not move with the input — the time scaffold — while per input the operation re-weights a recurring set of reader coordinates and the fact selects which stored row is read — the space circuit.

## Appendix B. Methods

**Native consumer.** Every language-model claim is read from the unchanged full-vocabulary logits or native greedy continuation; every diffusion claim from the native scheduler, latent bridge, VAE, and RGB conversion. No probe or replacement head is substituted for the consumer.

**Numerics.** Prospective runs use stock Hugging Face eager attention, CUDA, float32, tf32 off, seed 0, with the model loaded once and all branches run in process. The variance-decomposition specimens are Qwen2.5-1.5B (28 layers, 2 key/value heads) and Gemma-2-2B (26 layers, 4 key/value heads).

**Admission.** A context enters a quantitative panel only if the clean model answers it correctly among the candidates with a margin above a fixed threshold (clean top-1 equals the answer and the clean-minus-filler log-probability is at least 1.0). Non-admitted contexts are retained in the records and excluded from the panels; the admitted count is the effective n.

**Gates.** Each prospective cell passes an isolated no-op (overwriting a store with its own clean value changes the logits by less than 1e-3), a residual-write no-op, and a cache-path parity check (below 1e-3), or the cell is void. The variance-decomposition worker additionally reproduces the prior commit-layer and read-map canaries exactly before any new cell is scored.

**Site maps.** Store necessity is an isolated single-layer or whole-band key/value swap at the subject position with a clean prefix and native suffix. The read-site map is an isolated single cut at a (layer, key/value head, key-or-value) coordinate over the late band. The formation window is a single residual write of the clean subject state entering a block, measured as the log-probability gain of the written answer over the filler.

**Variance decomposition.** The read-site decomposition is a balanced attribution of the signed reader-profile variance to the coordinate main effect and its interactions with operation, lexical set, template, order, and binding, over a fully crossed design. The necessity decomposition is the same over operation, scene, order, and binding.
