# The Time Circuit Is the Scaffold: A Fixed Execution Schedule Hosts Per-Input Space Circuits

Jacob Hollenbeck

*Submission manuscript, 2026-10-02. Companion to [Time-Formed Circuits in Transformers](../time-formed-circuits/), which establishes the circuit class this paper reuses.*

## Abstract

A model builds the state controlling its answer over a forward pass: seeded, formed through ordered execution, committed to a late store, read by a consumer. We show this time circuit is a fixed scaffold. The location where the store forms and is read is set by architecture and training; it does not move when the fact, the operation, or the template changes. What changes per input is a space circuit on it: the operation re-weights a recurring reader, the fact selects which stored row is read. We hold a model fixed and decompose the variance of its causal site maps. In Qwen2.5-1.5B a crossed read-site map attributes 42.2% of variance to which coordinate is read and 29.0% to the operation's weighting, but 1.1% to the fact (1,152 cells); store necessity at the committed band is 84.0% operation and 3.2% fact (64 cells). Per fresh fact the commit layer is input-invariant: Gemma-2-2B commits at layer 22 (SD 0.00, 42 facts), Qwen2.5-1.5B at layer 23 (SD 1.16). A reader profile frozen before evaluation predicts held relation contexts in all eight; permuting the trained value-projection rows collapses it. The same organization appears in a state-space and a diffusion model; standard attribution tools find the space circuit and miss the scaffold. The cross-family common-graph claim ties a plain additive baseline — a null.

## 1. Introduction

A circuit in a neural network is usually read as a wiring diagram: a set of components whose activations, interchanged or ablated, change the output. The companion paper to this one shows that for factual recall this picture is incomplete. The state that controls the answer is not present at the input and read off by a fixed component; it is *built* across the layers of a forward pass, committed to a late store, and only then consumed. We call that construction a time circuit.

This paper makes a stronger architectural claim about the same object. The time circuit is a scaffold. Each trained model has a fixed, learned schedule — seed, then formation over ordered execution, then a commit band, then a store, then a consumer — and the location of each stage is a property of the architecture and the training, not of the input. The space circuit, by contrast, is chosen per forward pass: given the scaffold, the current operation decides how strongly each reader coordinate matters, and the current fact decides which stored row is read.

Figure 1 states the division. The upper track is fixed across inputs; the lower track is instantiated anew for each input on that fixed track. The contribution of this paper is to measure the two tracks apart, with a preregistered test, and to show that the standard attribution tools of the field see only the lower track.

![Figure 1. A fixed time scaffold hosts per-input space circuits. The upper track — seed, formation, commit band, store, consumer — has a location set by the architecture and its training and does not move with the input. The lower track is the space circuit: for each forward pass the fact selects which stored row is read, the operation weights a recurring set of reader coordinates, and the query decides which stored row has causal authority.](figures/fig1_scaffold_schematic.png)

The claim matters for interpretation. If the scaffold is fixed and the space circuit is dynamic, then a tool that attributes the answer to input-specific features near the embedding will find the dynamic part and be blind to the scaffold where the answer is actually committed. We show this directly: on a factual-recall behavior in Gemma-2-2B, a leading attribution-graph method places almost all subject influence at the first layer and sees at most 0.10 to 0.21 of the effect in the commit band, while a native cut of the committed store flips the answer 83% of the time.

The paper is organized around one decisive test and a ring of supporting evidence. Section 2 defines the scaffold, the space circuit, and the three grains at which the space circuit is dynamic. Sections 3 and 4 show, respectively, that the scaffold location is set by architecture and that the space circuit is chosen per input. Section 5 is the decisive test: a variance decomposition with preregistered falsifiers, none of which triggered. Section 6 shows the scaffold lives in the trained weights. Section 7 finds the same organization in a state-space model and a diffusion model. Section 8 shows that formed state feeds later computation, within bounds. Section 9 is an honest null: the cross-family common-graph claim that an earlier version of this work led with does not survive a discriminating test, and the thesis does not need it.

Every quantity is measured with the unchanged native consumer — full-vocabulary logits for language models, the native scheduler and decoder for diffusion — on stock Hugging Face eager execution in float32. Effective sample sizes are stated per claim, because the replication unit is the context, not the intervention. Where a claim rests on a single family or a single seed, the text says so.

## 2. Definitions

A time circuit is the construction, commitment, and consumption of the state that controls a model's answer, laid out along the model's ordered execution. For a transformer the ordering axis is layer depth; for a diffusion model it is the denoising step; for a state-space model it is the recurrent transition. This paper takes the existence and causal authority of that circuit as established by the companion work and asks what is fixed about it and what is free.

The **scaffold** is the fixed part. It is the schedule of stages — a seed, a formation window over ordered execution, a commit band, a store, and a consumer — together with the location of each stage in the model. The claim of this paper is that in a trained checkpoint these locations are properties of the architecture and the training: they are the same for different facts, different operations, and different sentence templates. The scaffold is not a constant tensor and it is not the engineered module graph; it is where, along execution, the controlling state forms and is read.

The **space circuit** is the free part. It is the response-specific computation instantiated on the scaffold for one forward pass: which stored row is read, with what payload, and how the reader is weighted. It is "space" in the sense of the companion taxonomy — a locally decisive operation at a position — but here it is explicitly hosted on the time scaffold rather than standing alone.

The space circuit is dynamic at three grains, which we keep separate because they carry different evidence.

1. **Row selection.** Which stored row the reader attends to and consumes. This is expected to move with the fact, because different facts live in different rows.
2. **Operation weighting.** How strongly each reader coordinate is weighted. This is expected to move with the operation — asking for identity versus a relation re-weights a recurring set of readers.
3. **Site rebuild.** Whether the set of read sites is genuinely different per input — a new local circuit each time. This is the grain the thesis predicts is *not* used: the reader is re-weighted and re-pointed, not rebuilt.

Two measurement terms recur. A **same-parent** intervention fixes a stored state and its intervention bytes exactly and changes only the surrounding native context, so that any behavioral difference is attributable to how the state is used rather than to a different state. **Query-selective harm** is the difference in a stored patch's effect on the answer when its fact is queried versus when the other fact is queried; a positive value means the identical bytes matter more when their fact is asked for. Throughout, a context is **admitted** only if the clean model answers it correctly with a margin above a fixed threshold; non-admitted contexts are retained in the records but excluded from the quantitative panels, and the admitted count is the effective n.

## 3. The scaffold is set by architecture

The clearest sign that the scaffold is architectural is that one architecture commits its store on exactly the layers its design distinguishes. In Gemma-2-2B, which interleaves sliding-window and global-attention layers, every layer that positively commits the factual store is a sliding-window layer. The four sliding layers spanning L16–L22 together carry 0.58 of the store's necessity, while individually they carry at most 0.08; the interleaved global layers L17 and L21 write slightly negative, from −0.03 to −0.08. Cutting the global layers alone *raises* the clean answer by 0.18. The architecture's own layer typing predicts where the commit lands (n = 8–42 admitted items per cell, single seed; Gemma-2-2B).

The commit sits at a recurring fractional depth across model sizes and families. In the Mamba state-space family one late writer dominates the commit at 130M (layer 20, depth 0.83, 81% of necessity), 370M (layer 39, depth 0.81, 67%), and 2.8B (layer 44, depth 0.69, 67% for identity), a span of roughly 0.69 to 0.83 of depth. Transformer late writers sit at about 0.7 to 0.9 of depth on the same measure. The commit is not at a fixed absolute layer; it is at a fixed place in the schedule (single seed; Mamba-130M/370M/2.8B and transformer sweeps).

The commit is a write port whose location is fixed by depth. A matched dose panel that separately scales retention and the input write at the Mamba writer layer finds that the write is the larger lever in all six size-by-task cells, and that releasing retention does not destroy the commit. The writer had been described as the layer that "stops its clock"; the causal test shows retention is a correlate of the commit, not its mechanism (single seed, three prompts per cell; Mamba-130M/370M/2.8B).

The store forms over a window that closes at the commit layer. A single residual write of the clean subject state, injected at a layer and then run forward natively, authors the correct answer from any layer up to the late writer and fails immediately after it: in Qwen2.5-0.5B the write gain is 0.70 at L21 and 0.04 at L22; in Pythia it is 0.48 at L17 then 0.01 at L18; in Mamba-130M it is 0.70 at L19 then 0.00 at L20. The formation window is a real interval with a hard closing edge, and the edge is the commit (single seed; four models).

Within that window the necessity is distributed, not localized. In Qwen2.5-1.5B identity the best single late layer carries only 0.08 of the store's necessity, the best three adjacent layers 0.23, and the six-layer band L22–L27 carries 0.96. No single layer owns the store; the band does. This is why a tool that scores single components underreads the scaffold (single seed; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

The decisive architectural measurement is that the commit layer does not move when the input changes. Figure 2 shows the commit layer measured per fresh fact by the closing edge of the formation window. In Gemma-2-2B every one of 42 identity facts commits at layer 22, a standard deviation of exactly 0.00. In Qwen2.5-1.5B the 42 identity facts commit at a median layer 23 with a standard deviation of 1.16 layers. The band's location is a constant of the checkpoint, measured fact by fact, not a per-input choice (stock HF eager, float32, single seed; Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B; 42 admitted identity facts each).

![Figure 2. The commit layer is input-invariant. Per fresh fact, measured by the closing edge of the formation window, Gemma-2-2B commits at layer 22 (SD 0.00 across 42 identity facts) and Qwen2.5-1.5B at layer 23 (SD 1.16). Across operations the commit shifts by 0 layers in Qwen2.5-1.5B and 2 in Gemma-2-2B. The scaffold location is a constant of the checkpoint.](figures/fig2_band_location.png)

The commit layer barely moves across operations either. The layer carrying peak single-layer necessity shifts by 0 layers between identity and color in Qwen2.5-1.5B, 1 in Qwen2.5-0.5B, and 2 in Gemma-2-2B, and the layer main effect accounts for 66% to 98% of the variance of the per-layer necessity curve. Whatever the model is asked, it commits in the same place (single seed; three transformer checkpoints).

## 4. The space circuit is chosen per input

The lead result that the space circuit is dynamic is a same-parent contrast: the same stored bytes acquire different causal authority under different questions. In Qwen2.5-1.5B we fix a late key/value store and its intervention bytes exactly — same donor state, same layer band, same positions, same tensor digests — and change only the native question. Figure 3 shows the query-selective harm. Asking for the patched fact rather than the other fact increases the patch's harm to the correct answer by a median 3.130 nats for color (range 2.066 to 5.565, positive in all 16 of 16 contrasts), 0.583 nats for position (0.424 to 1.302, 16/16), and 3.021 nats for a position-to-color lookup (2.236 to 4.772, 16/16). The identical stored state is consumed differently because the current query changes its use (32 unique same-parent query pairs; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

![Figure 3. The lead result: a byte-identical stored patch has query-dependent authority. Fixing the stored key/value state and its bytes exactly and changing only the native question, the patch's harm to the correct answer is larger when its own fact is queried — by a median 3.130 nats for color, 0.583 for position, and 3.021 for a position-to-color lookup, positive in all 16 of 16 contrasts each. The space circuit that consumes the store is chosen per input.](figures/fig3_same_patch_query.png)

The store also hosts several operations off one fixed interface. Replacing the queried late fact span with a coherent donor state redirects the native answer to the donor's color in 16/16 contexts and the donor's position in 12/16, and identity can additionally be redirected at the subject anchor, with the donor animal appearing in all 10 available continuations. One prior-fixed interface carries color, position, and identity reads; it does not follow that each read uses an identical microscopic circuit (single seed; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

The operation's role is to re-weight a recurring reader, not to rebuild it. Figure 4 shows the signed causal reader profile — the per-coordinate effect over the late key/value interface. Within one operation the profile recurs across different queried facts with cosine 0.947 for identity, 0.927 for color, and 0.961 for relation, and the same two value routes carry every operation. Averaging out single-measurement noise across fresh facts, the within-operation reader is essentially identical (cosine 0.92 to 0.999 in both Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B). The reader is a fixed object that operations tune (Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B).

![Figure 4. The operation re-weights a recurring reader; the fact does not rebuild it. Within one operation the signed reader profile recurs across facts with cosine 0.93 to 0.96 (0.92 to 0.999 after denoising fresh facts). Across operations it diverges — identity and relation share only cosine 0.336 — because identity loads one value route and relation the other. The reader is tuned by the operation, not rebuilt by the fact.](figures/fig4_reader_cosine.png)

Across operations the same reader diverges, because the operation selects which route to load. Identity and relation share only cosine 0.336 of their reader profile; identity loads the L23 key/value-head-0 value route with weight −1.18, relation loads the L22 key/value-head-1 route with −0.30, and color loads both. Operation weighting is the dominant dynamic grain (grain 2 of Section 2); identity-to-relation divergence is the cleanest case because the two operations load different routes (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

What moves with the fact is which row is read, not the reader. The raw attention mass over the target span across two facts has cosine 0.63, 0.61, and 0.70 for identity, color, and relation — markedly less stable than the intervention-effect reader profile, which holds at 0.95, 0.93, and 0.96. The selected rows move while the reader skeleton holds, which is exactly row selection (grain 1) over a fixed reader, not site rebuild (grain 3) (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

## 5. The variance decomposition: a preregistered test

The two tracks can be measured apart on one fixed model by decomposing the variance of its causal site maps. We preregistered the design, the metrics, and three falsifiers before collecting the prospective per-fact data, froze the predictions, and audited each falsifier against the artifacts afterward. The specimens are Qwen2.5-1.5B (28 layers, 2 key/value heads) and Gemma-2-2B (26 layers, 4 key/value heads), run on stock Hugging Face eager execution, float32, tf32 off, seed 0. Every cell passed its no-op and cache-parity gates, and the worker reproduces the prior commit-layer and read-map canaries exactly.

Figure 5 shows the read-site map decomposition. Over a fully crossed design in Qwen2.5-1.5B — 24 read coordinates × 3 operations × 2 lexical sets × 2 templates × 2 word orders × 2 queried bindings, 1,152 cells — the variance of the signed reader profile attributes 42.2% to which coordinate is read (the scaffold), 29.0% to the coordinate-by-operation interaction (operation weighting), and only 1.1% to the coordinate-by-fact interaction, 0.6% to the template, and 0.2% to the word order. The operation's weighting of the reader carries about 26 times the variance that the fact does. The read-site map is where, set by the scaffold, plus how, weighted by the operation; the fact and template each add about one percent.

![Figure 5. The variance decomposition. For the read-site map (left), which coordinate is read — the scaffold — carries 42.2% of variance and the operation's weighting 29.0%, while the fact carries 1.1% (Qwen2.5-1.5B, 1,152 cells). For store necessity at the committed band (right), the operation carries 84.0% and the fact 3.2% (64 cells). The scaffold and the operation dominate; the fact adds about one percent.](figures/fig5_variance_decomposition.png)

The store necessity at the committed band decomposes the same way. Replacing the whole late band and measuring the change in answer margin over a crossed operation × scene × order × binding design (64 cells) assigns 84.0% of the variance to the operation, 3.2% to the fact, 0.06% to the template, and 0.3% to the queried binding. How much store is needed is set overwhelmingly by the operation; the fact adds a few percent (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

The commit band location, measured directly per fresh fact, is the tightest term of all. Gemma-2-2B identity commits at layer 22 with standard deviation 0.00 across 42 facts; Qwen2.5-1.5B identity at median layer 23 with SD 1.16; Gemma-2-2B color at layer 20 with SD 1.11 across 25 facts. Across operations the median commit shifts by 0 layers in Qwen2.5-1.5B and 2 in Gemma-2-2B. The whole band is necessary — an isolated band swap changes the answer margin by more than half a nat — for 100% of admitted facts in every cell.

We preregistered three ways the thesis could fail, and none occurred. The band could have moved by more than four layers across facts; the largest standard deviation for a well-powered cell is 1.16 layers. The per-fact reader could have varied as much as the per-operation reader; the coordinate-by-fact term is 1.1% against the coordinate-by-operation term's 29.0%, and the denoised per-fact reader cosine is 0.92 to 0.999. The formation window could have depended on the input; its closing edge has standard deviation 0.00 to 1.16 layers and lies inside the necessity band for every fact. The one standard deviation above four layers — Qwen2.5-1.5B color, 4.17 — is produced by two weak-signal facts whose write authorship never rises much above threshold; the other 20 of 22 facts commit at layers 18–23, median 23, exactly where identity commits.

One confound in this prospective run must be stated plainly. Its three operations — identity, color, and shape — are all surface read-back, so on the read side they are too similar to separate operation from fact cleanly; the clean operation-versus-fact separation (coordinate-by-operation 29.0% versus coordinate-by-fact 1.1%, and the identity-to-relation reader cosine of 0.336) comes from the earlier panels that include a distinct relational operation. We rely on this run for the per-fact band location, which it establishes directly, and on the earlier panels for the operation-versus-fact read-map split.

## 6. The scaffold lives in the trained weights

If the scaffold is set by training, a profile of the reader frozen before evaluation should predict causal effects in contexts never opened, and disordering the trained weights that carry the reader should destroy it. Both hold. We froze a 24-coordinate signed reader profile over the late band, together with three competitors — an operation-blind mean profile, a wrong-operation profile, and a within-band coordinate permutation — before opening any held context, then evaluated on two lexical sets × two templates × two word orders. Figure 6 shows the result.

![Figure 6. The scaffold lives in the weights. A 24-coordinate reader profile frozen before evaluation predicts held relation contexts, beating three competitors (operation-blind, wrong-operation, coordinate-permutation) in all eight by raw error — mapped cosine 0.954 versus 0.566 operation-blind, raw RMSE 0.029 versus 0.149. Permuting all 256 rows of the L22/L23 value projections collapses the mapped cosine from 0.955 to 0.049 for relation, 0.838 to 0.110 for color, and 0.931 to 0.569 for identity. The measured profile depends on the trained value-projection row correspondence.](figures/fig6_confirmation_results.png)

The frozen profile predicts held relation contexts. For the relational operation the mapped profile beats every frozen competitor in raw error in all eight contexts — mapped cosine 0.954 against the operation-blind 0.566, mapped raw RMSE 0.029 against 0.149 — and its signed shape wins seven of eight contexts in the all-row view and all eight in the competence-conditioned view. The eight contexts are two lexical sets crossed with two templates and two orders, which are correlated replications rather than eight independent worlds; the prediction is prospective but its effective breadth is modest (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

The prediction is operation-specific, not uniform. Color retains the broad profile shape but misses its coarse reader ordering, winning only five of eight contexts, and the operation-blind profile has slightly lower median error there. Identity is readout-limited: most native identity continuations use a format the fixed readout does not score, and in the four contexts with a competent identity continuation the operation-blind comparator is better (0 of 4). Size never produces a native answer in the frozen format and is unavailable. The scaffold's reader transfers prospectively for the relational operation and partially for color; it is not a single uniform profile across operations (Qwen2.5-1.5B).

Disordering the trained value projections collapses the profile. Mild permutations of 5% and 20% of the rows of the L22 and L23 value projections leave the signed profile largely intact, but permuting all 256 rows of both projections, with their biases, changes the mapped cosine from 0.955 to 0.049 for relation, 0.838 to 0.110 for color, and 0.931 to 0.569 for identity. Native competence falls under the full permutation as well, so this is a dependence result, not a behavior-matched null: the measured reader depends on the final-checkpoint row correspondence of two specific value projections. It does not date when that organization was acquired (four opened contexts, post-hoc; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

## 7. The same organization in a state-space model and a diffusion model

The scaffold is a functional organization, not a tensor, so it should appear in architectures with different state interfaces. It does. In Mamba-130M one shared recurrent state serves two operations: reading a country's identity and reading its capital are both carried by the same late state. Delivering only the successor state's content changes the later answer in all 12 qualified rows, and a frozen 27-coordinate profile predicts two held country pairs — capital in all eight rows (mapped cosine 0.9941 against the operation-blind 0.9880) and delayed identity in all four (0.9586 against 0.9102). One recurrent store, read two ways, with the reader re-weighted by operation, is the same division as in the transformer (single seed; Mamba-130M).

The Mamba commit is likewise a depth-fixed write port, as Section 3 reported: the write dose is the causal lever in all six size-by-task cells, and retention is a correlate. The shared state's reads are operation-dependent, and the clock-dose and reader effects change with the operation and the token boundary, which is the space circuit varying on a fixed carried-state scaffold (single seed; Mamba-130M/370M/2.8B).

Diffusion shows the scaffold as a fixed bus carrying a per-prompt program. In text-to-image models the prompt compiles into a static key/value program and the live latent issues a dynamic query; installing the static source program reproduces the native pixel output byte-for-byte in 21 of 21 cases across seven specimens and five diffusion families, including prompts never used to build the tooling, while wrong-source and wrong-sign controls diverge by 57 to 97 in mean absolute error. The machinery is prompt-agnostic; the program it carries is not — four new scene families reused the same physical route but ran different logical topologies (7 specimens, 5 families).

The timing of a write on the diffusion scaffold decides which kind of edit it produces, which is the diffusion analogue of the formation window. Installing the same source state early in denoising changes composition and framing; installing it late changes iris color while preserving composition. A four-case reproduction reached violet eyes with a late write and left them blue with an early write, reproducing 12 native references exactly across 56 images. Where on the schedule the space circuit acts determines what it can change (single model, four matched cases).

## 8. Formed state feeds later computation

The scaffold is not only read once; state formed on it can feed a later step. We bound this claim carefully, because its effective n is small. In Qwen2.5-1.5B, in a development organism and in one of three held city/country contexts, the model's own intermediate prediction becomes a second formed store that determines a later answer. With the first formed state, the visible intermediate token, the prefix, the later query, and the recipient's final hidden state all fixed, replacing only the second formed row changes the later answer from France to Germany; zeroing that row breaks the later answer to a function word; and exact reinstatement restores it. The other two held contexts are informative but do not earn the semantic claim — one is causal but not competent, one fails to form the target — so the competent held n is 1 of 3 (single seed; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

A separate test shows a formed payload is consumed by a later reference operation. A development-frozen late payload store, carrying one entity's value, changes the native authorship of that entity in 7 of 8 held reference operations while preserving the other entity in all 8; when a reference points at the changed store the later answer follows the new value in 3 of 4 cases, and when it points at the preserved store it keeps the old value in all 4. Formed state has downstream authority at the reference, with the pointer-versus-copy question left open (two fresh values crossed with two orders; Qwen2.5-1.5B).

The companion paper carries the quantitative source-to-store mediation that underpins this section, and we cite it rather than re-deriving it: blocking the recipient-formed late band removes a median 0.924 of an upstream seed's answer-gain, and transplanting that band into an unseeded recipient recovers 0.871, across 42 rows, with a wrong identity's band authoring the wrong identity in 37 of 42. These results establish the store as a mediator; this paper's contribution is that the store, once formed, is read by a per-input space circuit.

## 9. What does not generalize

An earlier version of this work led with a cross-family common role graph — a single scaffold shared across transformers, state-space models, and diffusion models. That claim does not survive a discriminating test, and we report it as a null. The thesis of this paper — a fixed scaffold per model, with per-input space circuits on it — does not need a cross-family common graph, and is not weakened by its failure. Figure 7 shows the two decisive nulls.

![Figure 7. The cross-family common-graph claim is a null. Left: a connected scaffold forecast ties a plain additive baseline exactly (MAE 0.015099 versus 0.015099) and is slightly worse than operation-blind and query-only controls (0.014593, 0.014588), winning only 1 of 3 contexts. Right: the best common role-matcher costs 6.9125 against a depth null's 6.9511, a gap of 0.0386. On these tests a shared cross-family graph cannot be told apart from addition or from matched depth.](figures/fig7_honest_null.png)

A connected cross-family forecast ties plain addition exactly. Predicting the held connected effect from the scaffold gives a mean absolute error of 0.015099 logit units, against 0.014593 for an operation-blind constant and 0.014588 for a query-only account, and it ties a four-feature additive baseline at 0.015099 to the digit. It wins only 1 of 3 held contexts against each control. On this test the scaffold forecast cannot be distinguished from addition (3 held contexts; Qwen2.5-1.5B route to a recipient successor).

A common role-matcher is indistinguishable from a depth null. The best alignment of roles across families costs 6.9125 against the next assignment's 6.9511 and a depth null nearly matched, a gap of 0.0386. It uses opened outcomes and differently resolved assays and cannot establish a unique cross-family alignment.

Two transfers also miss. The color reader ordering predicted from the profile misses in all eight held contexts, even though color's full 24-coordinate profile remains partially predictive. And the prospective cross-family core that does align — the single edge from formed carried state to the unchanged native consumer, where the mean profiles of the two families match at RMSE 0.0928 against 0.8526 under a permuted-role map — rests on an effective sample of two diffusion rows, two state-space rows, and one competent transformer row. That is a suggestive common edge, not a shared graph, and we state it as such.

The honest reading is that the within-model scaffold is strong and prospectively confirmed, while the across-model common graph is not. The paper's claims are the former.

## 10. Related work

Factual recall has a well-developed localization literature. Meng et al. locate factual associations in mid-layer MLPs and edit them, and Geva et al. separate subject enrichment, relation propagation, and attention-mediated extraction into stages ([Meng et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.05262); [Geva et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14767)). Our contribution is orthogonal to where a fact lives: we show that the location at which the controlling state commits is input-invariant, while the reader that consumes it is re-weighted per operation and re-pointed per fact. Hase et al. and Agarwal note that localization and editing can come apart and that recall defers commitment; our formation-window and commit-band measurements give that deferral a fixed schedule ([Hase et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04213); [Agarwal 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17537)).

Context-dependent binding and reusable operation carriers are the closest predecessors to the space-circuit side of our account. Feng and Steinhardt identify binding-identity representations through causal interventions, and Todd et al. and Hendel et al. show that a reusable operation can be carried as a compact vector in model state ([Feng and Steinhardt 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191); [Todd et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15213); [Hendel et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15916)). Our same-parent assay adds a different question — how identical stored bytes acquire different authority under a changed query — and our variance decomposition shows the reader is re-weighted rather than rebuilt. We do not claim every dynamic circuit is a compact vector.

Attribution-graph and automated-circuit methods are the tools our scaffold is partly invisible to. Automated circuit discovery and prompt-local attribution graphs with interpretable replacement models recover input-specific feature graphs, and attention-level tracing decomposes reads through query-key feature interactions ([Conmy et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997); [Ameisen et al. 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html); [Kamath et al. 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attention-qk/index.html); [Lindsey et al. 2025](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html)). The companion paper shows directly that one such method, on a recall behavior in Gemma-2-2B, places most subject influence at the first layer and sees at most 0.10 to 0.21 of the effect in the commit band while a native store cut flips the answer 83% of the time, with about 30% of influence running through error nodes. These tools find the per-input space circuit; the scaffold is in the state they treat as an endpoint. Geiger et al.'s causal-abstraction framework supplies the standard against which our role graph is a candidate abstraction with incomplete alignment ([Geiger et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02997)).

Diffusion and state-space interpretability supply the cross-architecture tests. Cross-attention editing and transcoder-based diffusion tracing establish that prompt binding and temporal organization are addressable, and the Pythia and Mamba model families supply developmental and recurrent specimens ([Hertz et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01626); [Mazur et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15796); [Biderman et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01373); [Gu and Dao 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752)). Self-repair and the Hydra effect explain why a single removed component is often repaired downstream, which is why we measure whole bands rather than single layers ([McGrath et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15771)).

## 11. Limitations

The scope is two transformer checkpoints, one small state-space model, and one diffusion family, at a single seed under a prototype-first policy. The variance decomposition and the band-location result are measured on Qwen2.5-1.5B and Gemma-2-2B; the state-space results are Mamba-130M with sweeps to 2.8B; the diffusion results are FLUX and SDXL. This bounds a stable scaffold with per-input space circuits in these models; it is not a universal-architecture proof, and the cross-family common graph of Section 9 is an explicit null.

Effective sample sizes are small and correlated, and the replication unit is the context, not the intervention. The prospective relation prediction rests on eight correlated contexts from two lexical sets; the formed-state feedback result is competent in 1 of 3 held contexts; the cross-family common edge is two plus two plus one rows. The per-fact band-location result is the best-powered claim, at 42 admitted identity facts per model. Where a number is a single-measurement cosine we denoise by averaging within a template and say so; the raw per-item read-map cosines of 0.72 to 0.90 are noise, not a weaker signal.

One instrument defect is worth stating precisely, because it bears on how much of this work to trust. An earlier feedback instrument registered a custom attention function without its matching mask, so a multi-token query chunk could attend to future tokens — a correctness failure that nonetheless passed the instrument's own replay checks. That defect was confined to a retired selector-and-feedback instrument that this paper does not rely on. It does not touch the results here: the variance decomposition, the same-parent query contrast, the frozen-profile prediction, the formation-window and commit-band sweeps, and the France-to-Germany feedback all run on stock Hugging Face eager execution, and a corrected re-run of the affected routing measurement reproduced its qualitative effect under untouched eager execution with zero future attention in every preceding query row. The lesson we take is that a verification apparatus certifies that an instrument ran, not that its result is valid, and that the headline claims of this paper should be re-derived by independent groups on stock execution.

Several mechanistic questions remain open. We localize the store to a band and the reader to a set of coordinates, but not to a minimal set of heads or to the attention-versus-MLP split of the writer. We show the query re-points the reader but do not trace the query-to-row addressing algorithm, and a conventional learned lookup remains a compatible implementation. And the weight-permutation result ties the reader to the final checkpoint without dating when the organization was acquired; a same-assay checkpoint trajectory is the direct test and is not yet run.

## 12. Conclusion

The time circuit that controls a model's factual answer is a fixed scaffold. In a trained checkpoint the store forms and is read at a location set by the architecture and the training, and that location does not move when the fact, the operation, or the template changes. We measured this by holding a model fixed and decomposing the variance of its causal site maps: which coordinate is read and how the operation weights it dominate the read-site map at 42.2% and 29.0%, the operation dominates store necessity at 84.0%, and the commit layer is constant per fresh fact to a standard deviation of 0.00 in Gemma-2-2B and 1.16 in Qwen2.5-1.5B, with three preregistered falsifiers none of which triggered.

The space circuit is chosen per input on that scaffold. Identical stored bytes carry different authority under different questions, by a median 3.130 nats for color; the operation re-weights a recurring reader whose within-operation profile recurs at cosine 0.92 to 0.999 while across operations it diverges to 0.336; and the fact moves which row is read, not the reader. A reader profile frozen before evaluation predicts held relation contexts in all eight, and permuting the trained value-projection rows collapses it, so the scaffold lives in the weights. The same organization — one store, read several ways, with a depth-fixed commit — appears in a state-space model and in a diffusion model's prompt-agnostic bus.

The consequence for interpretation is concrete. Standard attribution tools find the input-specific features near the embedding, which is the space circuit, and are nearly blind to the committed store, which is the scaffold. A circuit account that stops at the per-input graph describes the part of the computation the input chooses and misses the part the architecture fixes. The honest boundary is that this holds within a model: the cross-family common graph that would make the scaffold universal ties a plain additive baseline and is reported as a null. Within that boundary, the architecture is the time circuit, and it works as a scaffold.

## Appendix B. Methods

**Native consumer.** Every language-model claim is read from the unchanged full-vocabulary logits or native greedy continuation; every diffusion claim from the native scheduler, latent bridge, VAE, and RGB conversion. No probe or replacement head is substituted for the consumer.

**Numerics.** Prospective runs use stock Hugging Face eager attention, CUDA, float32, tf32 off, seed 0, with the model loaded once and all branches run in process. The variance-decomposition specimens are Qwen2.5-1.5B (28 layers, 2 key/value heads) and Gemma-2-2B (26 layers, 4 key/value heads).

**Admission.** A context enters a quantitative panel only if the clean model answers it correctly among the candidates with a margin above a fixed threshold (clean top-1 equals the answer and the clean-minus-filler log-probability is at least 1.0). Non-admitted contexts are retained in the records and excluded from the panels; the admitted count is the effective n.

**Gates.** Each prospective cell passes an isolated no-op (overwriting a store with its own clean value changes the logits by less than 1e-3), a residual-write no-op, and a cache-path parity check (below 1e-3), or the cell is void. The variance-decomposition worker additionally reproduces the prior commit-layer and read-map canaries exactly before any new cell is scored.

**Site maps.** Store necessity is an isolated single-layer or whole-band key/value swap at the subject position with a clean prefix and native suffix. The read-site map is an isolated single cut at a (layer, key/value head, key-or-value) coordinate over the late band. The formation window is a single residual write of the clean subject state entering a block, measured as the log-probability gain of the written answer over the filler.

**Variance decomposition.** The read-site decomposition is a balanced attribution of the signed reader-profile variance to the coordinate main effect and its interactions with operation, lexical set, template, order, and binding, over a fully crossed design. The necessity decomposition is the same over operation, scene, order, and binding.
