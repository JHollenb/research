# Intent-reader review: time-formed circuits and functional scaffolds

**Reader:** GPT-5.6 Sol, high reasoning  
**Date:** 2026-10-01  
**Drafts reviewed:** `space-and-time-circuits/time-emphasis-paper.md` and `scaffolds-and-dynamic-circuits/bridge-paper.md`  
**Scope:** first-read meaning, conceptual realization, narrative fit to the author's intended message, ambiguity, distracting emphasis, and actionable prose/structure changes. This is a read-only review of the working papers; no manuscript edits or new experiments were requested or performed.

## Overall judgment

The two-paper sequence has a real conceptual center, and I found it before reading the supplied intent. The first paper's strongest realization is that apparently competing localization results can be different stages of one causal anatomy: an upstream source can initiate a native construction process, later state can become independently causally authoritative, and a downstream read can consume it through a more local port. Asking only “where is the circuit?” collapses source, formation, committed state, and use. That is the missing piece.

The second paper's strongest realization is similarly clear: the first paper explains how a causal record comes into being; the second asks what organization can be reused while the record's content and current use change. The same stored intervention matters differently under a changed native question. This makes a functional scaffold intelligible as reusable roles and interfaces, while the dynamic circuit is the execution-specific payload, read, support, dose, and timing active now.

Both realizations are present and well supported in the opening sections. In the latest live draft, the first paper delivers the intended “that was the missing piece” moment clearly through Figure 1, the operational dissociation in §2.1, and the paragraph distinguishing residual source, isolated K/V state, and read intervention. The bridge clicks in its first two paragraphs. The later bodies weaken those moments by accumulating support taxonomies, family variants, evidentiary qualifications, and secondary experiments faster than the reader can keep the central explanatory picture in view.

My main recommendation is therefore editorial, not experimental: preserve the strong causal content, but make one explanatory sentence govern each paper. For paper one: **a mechanism may be local at its write or read port while still being formed through an ordered native process; source, formation, committed state, and consumption are different causal questions.** For paper two: **models can reuse a functional organization while prompts, queries, and carried history instantiate different causal subcircuits within it.** Every major section should visibly advance one of those sentences.

## First-read account of paper one

### What I thought it claimed before seeing the stated intent

I read the paper as proposing an operational class of circuits whose identity depends partly on causal history. In the main Qwen example, a supplied residual is not itself the final stored answer record. It initiates unchanged computation that produces later K/V state; blocking that recipient-formed state removes most of the source effect, and transplanting it into another recipient restores most of the effect. The paper then argues that the source port, formation interval, committed store, and consumer read can have different spatial support.

The most important consequence is that local and distributed descriptions need not conflict. A single local residual edit can launch a longer construction; a later local K/V transplant can bypass that construction; a concentrated read can access state whose necessity is distributed across a wider late band. These interventions answer different causal questions.

I took diffusion to be a complementary case, not the definition of the class. The strict FLUX assay demonstrates one support topology in which the complete temporal path meets declared behavioral criteria and every tested singleton does not. The eye edit illustrates that the causal authority of a supplied operand depends on when it is installed into the carried history.

### The conceptual realization I carried away

The paper changed “circuit localization” from a single-coordinate question into a staged causal anatomy. The central realization is not merely that computation unfolds over time. It is that the state later consumed by the model can be **formed**, can acquire persistent causal authority at a declared interface, and can then be **read**, with each stage exposing a different intervention profile. This explains why early restoration, late state transplantation, and localized reading can all look decisive without identifying the same object.

That realization matches the supplied intent closely. The paper clearly supports source → ordered native formation → committed state → consumer. It also clearly states that local ports and temporally or spatially distributed support can coexist. The latest draft defines commitment as acquisition of independent causal authority rather than cache persistence, says the internal formation path remains unresolved, and separates the Qwen explanatory result from the FLUX subtype certificate. The word “hardening” is correctly bounded in §7 as persistent influence rather than weight modification or total irreversibility. The diffusion eye example is also correctly described as a target-assisted transplant of the complete contextual text stream at selected calls, with collateral, rather than as a reusable iris module.

### Where the realization becomes less sharp

1. **The abstract still presents several contributions before it states the paradox the anatomy resolves.** It is now conceptually accurate and uses “ordered formation interval,” but it remains a compact findings inventory. The reader learns the numbers, isolated-store correction, strict subtype, and eye edit before the locality/formation hinge is stated in plain terms.

2. **The paper gives the reader the answer to “where is the circuit?” but could state the resolved contradiction more concretely and earlier.** §8 now names the distinction clearly. Moving a compressed version into §1 would let the reader immediately see that an early residual source, a wider late necessity band with strong local write ports, and a concentrated read can all belong to one mechanism because they measure construction, stored authority, and consumption.

3. **The eye illustration is responsibly qualified, but a skimming reader still encounters “changes iris color” before “complete target-derived contextual text stream.”** Put the full-stream, target-assisted qualification in the main sentence immediately before the image, not only in the caption and later §7. The intended dissociation is timing versus visible feature/composition effect, not discovery of an iris variable.

4. **The spatial/temporal taxonomy is more open than the confident subtype vocabulary can make it seem.** The paper correctly says locality and temporal formation are separate axes and allows local, distributed, and mixed support. It should add one direct sentence that these examples do not yet supply a complete taxonomy of dynamic support across space, layer depth, denoising time, and recurrent transitions.

### Precise revision moves for paper one

- Open the abstract with the resolved contradiction: “A transformer mechanism can have a local write or read port while the state used there is formed through a longer ordered computation. We call this causal organization a time-formed circuit.” Then retain the current source → native formation interval → committed state → consumer anatomy before giving results.
- Add one sentence at the end of §1: “The apparent localization conflict is therefore expected: source writes, formed-store interventions, and read interventions localize different stages of one circuit.”
- Introduce the eye image with: “This is a target-assisted transplant of the target run's complete contextual text stream at selected calls; it illustrates timing-dependent control and collateral, not an iris-specific module.” Keep the existing quantitative and visual qualifications.
- Move the sentence declaring the taxonomy open into §2.2, after local/path-distributed/mixed support are introduced.
- Consider moving some detailed variant and instrument material from §§4, 8, and 9 into a compact “boundary profiles and evidence scope” section. The goal is to let the Qwen causal chain remain the paper's spine while retaining all records.

## First-read account of the bridge paper

### What I thought it claimed before seeing the stated intent

I read the bridge as asking what remains reusable once the first paper has established that contextual causal state is formed and consumed. Its answer is a functional role organization: sources provide context-specific operands; live queries or transitions select and transform them; writers commit state; later native consumers use it. The physical sites and payloads can vary while the roles recur.

The Qwen same-parent result made this idea concrete. A byte-identical K/V patch in the same parent store has different consequences when only the native question changes. Thus the patch has no fixed, query-independent behavioral authority. Its causal participation is selected by the live operation. The paper then proposes that smaller response-specific circuits inhabit reusable interfaces, with diffusion, Mamba, and developmental Pythia as family-local evidence and boundary cases.

### The conceptual realization I carried away

The scaffold is the reusable organization of possible work; the dynamic circuit is the particular work enacted for this context and operation. Prompt-built state does not need to be a permanent semantic module. A formed record can sit in a recurring interface, while a live query makes only some of it causally relevant for the current response.

This does produce the intended “it clicks” moment. The first two paragraphs of §1 are excellent because they move directly from the same patch/different question result to a simple division of labor: the first paper follows construction of the record; the second follows its use across operations. The phrase “rather than a permanent semantic module assigned to one word” is especially helpful.

### Where the click is lost

1. **“Scaffold” expands into several claims that are not equally supported.** The draft moves among a reused causal interface, recurring roles across contexts, a carried-state feedback graph, learned organization across training, and a possible cross-family role graph. The abstract carefully calls the shared learned recurrent graph a working inference, but the body repeatedly uses one scaffold vocabulary for all levels. A reader needs an explicit claim ladder.

2. **“Recurrent” has two meanings in §2.** Responsibilities recurring across executions and carried state feeding the next operation are distinct. The first is reuse; the second is feedback. Calling both recurrence makes the central architecture harder to test and creates needless ambiguity beside recurrent state-space models.

3. **The role vocabulary changes faster than the reader can stabilize it.** The first paper has source, formation, committed store, consumer. The bridge adds operation/address, carrier/transition, writer, carried state, then the record `(A,P,alpha,tau,M)`, and later source/live address/transition, contextual carrier, recurring writer, and native consumer. These may all be valid distinctions, but the paper needs one canonical role diagram and a case-mapping table before adding refinements.

4. **The evidence supports selective consumption more directly than a full scaffold.** The same-patch experiment establishes that a live query changes the causal authority of fixed stored state. It remains compatible with conventional query-dependent lookup over a reused fact store. The draft states this caveat well. The conclusion should keep “reused causal interface with query-conditioned consumption” as the measured result and “learned functional scaffold” as the architectural working inference.

5. **The evidence inventory after the Qwen section obscures the simple mechanism statement.** SDXL factorials, source-launch/refinement grammar, writer transport, scene editing, E1 scope diagnostics, Mamba operands, Pythia development, and E4 alignment all matter, but together they make the reader repeatedly reconstruct what “scaffold” means. Several are best presented as a single cross-family mapping table plus short boundary paragraphs.

6. **“Smaller circuit” is intuitive but underspecified.** The text says “small” is functional rather than dimensional, which avoids a false neuron-count claim but risks making the phrase unfalsifiable. Use “execution-specific causal coalition at the declared intervention grain” or simply “response-specific causal support,” and give one Qwen and one diffusion example.

7. **The time/space taxonomy remains open here too.** The bridge should explicitly inherit the first paper's separation: reusable local ports, distributed formation, and context-specific support can coexist. The current evidence does not yet determine a complete taxonomy of whether dynamic support is spatially local, temporally distributed, both, or mixed for each role.

### Precise revision moves for the bridge

- Keep the first two paragraphs of §1 as the narrative anchor. End them with a compact definition: “A functional scaffold is the recurring organization of roles and interfaces; a dynamic circuit is the execution-specific causal support that carries this prompt's payload into this operation.”
- Add a four-level claim ladder near the end of §1:
  1. **Measured:** the same late interface supports multiple contents and operations, and a fixed patch has query-dependent native effects.
  2. **Convergent trend:** source/read/write/state/consumer responsibilities recur in family-local forms.
  3. **Working inference:** those responsibilities form a learned reusable scaffold, including state feedback.
  4. **Open:** a portable cross-family role graph and a full spatial/temporal taxonomy.
- Rename the two meanings of recurrence. Use **reuse across executions** for recurring functional roles and **carried-state feedback** for a state influencing a later read/write. Reserve “loop” for evidence that follows the altered state through a subsequent write into newly testable state.
- Use one canonical role sequence aligned with paper one, for example: `contextual source -> native formation/transform -> committed state -> query-conditioned read/consumer`, with writer and carried register shown only where the assay distinguishes them.
- Add a compact case table with rows for Qwen, SDXL/FLUX, and Mamba and columns for supplied source, native formation/transform, committed state, live read/operation, consumer, and current evidence limit. This would replace repeated prose explanations and make “family-local lowering” immediately legible.
- Compress §4's diffusion evidence into one main example for live binding (fixed K/V, changing Q), one for commitment/history (program × carried register), and one explicit boundary note about the target-assisted whole-stream eye transplant. Move remaining details to the ledger or a compact evidence table.
- Compress Mamba/Pythia into “family-local and developmental boundary evidence” unless the body is making a distinct scaffold claim from them. Their current detail interrupts the Qwen-to-scaffold line more than it advances it.
- Replace “smaller circuits” in the section title and first claim with “response-specific causal support” or define the declared intervention grain at which smaller is meant.
- Add one explicit sentence: “The present evidence establishes coexistence of reusable interfaces, temporally formed state, and context-dependent support; it does not yet yield an exhaustive spatial/temporal taxonomy of dynamic circuits.”

## Cross-paper continuity

The intended division of labor is sound and already visible:

```text
Paper 1: How does consumer-relevant state come into being and acquire causal authority?
         source -> ordered native formation -> committed state -> consumer

Paper 2: What can remain reusable while the current content and operation change?
         recurring roles/interfaces + prompt/query/history-specific causal support
```

The most useful cross-paper change would be to repeat exactly one shared diagram and vocabulary. Paper one should end with the established anatomy and ask which roles recur. Paper two should begin with that same anatomy, visually hold the roles fixed, then show content/query/support changing across executions. This would make the companion relation felt rather than merely stated.

The papers should also share one scope sentence: a local source, store, or read port can coexist with distributed or temporally extended formation, and the current examples do not exhaust the possible spatial/temporal support profiles. That sentence protects the intended view from being mistaken for a binary “space circuits versus time circuits” taxonomy.

## Prioritized recommendations

1. **Lead each abstract with its one explanatory realization.** Paper one resolves apparent localization conflict through source/formation/commitment/consumer anatomy. Paper two distinguishes reusable role organization from execution-specific causal support.
2. **Add an evidence ladder to the bridge.** Reused interface and query-conditioned consumption are measured; a learned recurrent scaffold, complete feedback graph, and portable cross-family role graph remain progressively stronger inferences.
3. **Unify the role vocabulary and diagram across both papers.** Use one anatomy, then map Qwen, diffusion, and Mamba onto it with their limits.
4. **Reduce body-level evidence inventory.** Preserve all records, but move secondary numeric detail and family variants into concise tables or boundary sections so the causal story remains visible.
5. **Qualify the eye example before the image in main prose.** State target assistance, whole contextual stream, selected calls, and collateral before the reader sees an apparent iris edit.
6. **State that the spatial/temporal taxonomy remains open.** The established result is compatibility and several bounded profiles, not a complete partition of dynamic circuits.

The latest paper-one draft already fixes three issues that would otherwise have been high priority: it defines the class by a source/store causal dissociation rather than generic execution order, defines commitment as independent causal authority rather than cache persistence, and makes Qwen the primary mechanism result while bounding FLUX to the strict subtype certificate. Those distinctions should be preserved through further editing.

With those changes, the first paper should leave a reader with a durable correction to the usual localization question, and the bridge should feel like the natural next step rather than a second evidence dossier.
