**Review date:** 2026-10-01, America/Los_Angeles.  
**Snapshot:** updated draft 2, including the October 1 E8, E9, E10 and E18 additions.  
**Manuscript SHA-256:** `0a3ba5eab96a997234b680fed70cf4d25d879d169ab03f087fa958dec6ff8ceb`.  
**Reviewers:** primary evidence/argument audit, plus three explicitly requested GPT-5.6 Sol subagents at high reasoning for language-model, diffusion, and tools/CoT prior art.  
**Disposition:** substantial, attention-worthy research; major revision of claims and presentation before submission. The manuscript and historical receipts were not edited.

## Overall assessment and grade

There is a good paper here. Its strongest contribution is a comparative causal anatomy: distributed necessity can coexist with concentrated authorship; the answer becomes available through a residual write before a late formed-state commit/read; and the same intervention matrix reveals different structures in diffusion, transformers, and recurrent models. The updated full sweeps, isolated-write correction, window cuts, Mamba retention measurements, mixed SDXL result, and non-copied intermediate-state experiment make this substantially stronger than the earlier sampled-certificate story.

I would grade the **research package B+ / about 8 out of 10**, and the **manuscript as currently written B- / about 7 out of 10 overall**. Its prose/claim consistency alone is closer to 6 out of 10. These are subjective reviewer judgments, not calibrated acceptance probabilities or a field percentile. With the existing evidence organized around accurate claims, an A- assessment of the bounded contribution is plausible; that is not a promise of acceptance.

The main weakness is not insufficient total evidence. Several decisive headline statements are stronger than the measurements, contradicted elsewhere in the draft, or already partly anticipated by prior work. Those are repairable. Adding a huge benchmark before correcting them would do less for the paper than clarifying the mechanism and publishing its smallest reproducible core.

**Frontier-lab answer: yes, the findings are strong enough to merit attention from relevant researchers at Anthropic and OpenAI.** The causal state tools, necessity/write asymmetry, commit/read alignment, and measured transcoder-basis blind spot are credible reasons to inspect the work. This is a judgment about scientific relevance, not a prediction that either lab will respond, endorse it, or recruit the author. The demonstrated CoT result is an exact causal audit primitive; general CoT tracking remains a promising next step.

## Major critique 1: the four quadrants do not uniquely certify a new mechanism

At **paper line 166**, the draft asserts that a diffuse linear representation fails the whole-path quadrants and therefore cannot produce the cross. That assertion is false under the paper's quantitative rules.

Consider an unchanged linear consumer:

```
y = (x1 + x2 + ... + x8) / 8
source: all xi = 0
target: all xi = 1
```

Replacing one target component with the source removes 0.125 of the effect; writing one target component into the source adds 0.125. Replacing/writing the whole set removes/recovers 1.0. Thus it passes single necessity <0.25, single write <0.2, and whole write ≥0.9. If behavior is the class `y > 0.5`, each singleton leaves the class unchanged and the complete intervention changes it. No temporal order, recurrent accumulation, or distinct “time” mechanism is required. The audit record includes this counterexample.

This does **not** disprove the FLUX finding. It disproves the claim that the certificate alone distinguishes that mechanism from distributed additive carriage plus the chosen consumer/threshold. Redundancy, adaptive repair, saturation, and interaction can also produce parts of the pattern.

The straightforward repair is to call the matrix an **operational signature at a declared cut and measurement resolution**. Use ordering, dose, window, read/write, and mediation results to infer the mechanism. The paper already contains more informative temporal evidence: equal nominal dose delivered at different times behaves differently, and net-zero ordering can reverse the effect. Those observations should carry the temporal interpretation.

Also repair the definitions:

- “Space” currently includes any measurable singleton effect, while “time” permits sizeable subthreshold singleton effects. FLUX's best singleton moves roughly 0.37–0.46 along the dp effect axis while retaining the target image class. That is dispensability for a declared outcome, not an inert component.
- The four-quadrant definition is conjunctive, but the draft repeatedly labels necessity-only or sufficiency-only cases “time circuits.” Use explicit quadrant descriptions for mixed cells.
- State which axis is execution time, depth, stored-layer index, or generated-token position. A transformer cache contains simultaneously available layer-specific entries; that is related to execution history but is not automatically the same physical mechanism as recurrent denoising accumulation.
- Keep necessity and sufficiency comparisons at the same cut when classifying a cell. Residual writes that propagate through later layers and isolated formed-K/V writes answer different questions. The draft's new distinction is valuable precisely because these interventions differ.

A useful name can be introduced without claiming that the matrix proves an exhaustive ontology of neural circuits. “Two intervention phenotypes and their mixed cases” is supported more directly than “there are two classes and one certificate to tell them apart.”

## Major critique 3: the ledger is interesting, but not a conservation law as written

The Mamba agreement is worth highlighting: the late-half residual-write curve closely tracks the sum of downstream state-write effects. The reported discrepancies are 0.02–0.15, with layer-write totals 0.94–1.09. Calling that exact conservation is inaccurate, especially when effects are normalized to order one.

Transformers show high shape correlation, but isolated singleton-write totals range up to 2.96. The result is a **commit profile with redundant/non-additive authorship**, not equality. The post-commit bound “0.001–0.15” also has an exception in the E17 findings table: **Gemma color is 0.208**. Either state the exception or define precisely which cells/indices are excluded; do not change the receipt.

Moreover, these are sums of normalized behavioral gains, often layerwise medians. They are not a tensor conservation identity. A sum of medians need not equal the median of a sum, and logprob-normalized effects are not generally additive under nonlinear consumers. Shared late-depth trends can also yield high Pearson correlation. The arithmetic is useful evidence for a working decomposition; it does not by itself identify independent causal commits.

One small future discriminant would be to predict a few held-out joint layer masks or conditional increments from the ledger, preserving signs and errors. That would distinguish predictive accounting from a descriptive cumulative curve. It is optional for a bounded descriptive claim; it becomes necessary if the paper insists on a conservation-law claim.

Similarly, “the clock causes storage” needs a controlled clock intervention. Current Mamba evidence shows retention and authoring concentrate at the same layer. A small same-layer clock-hold/release comparison, with matched content and downstream continuation, would strengthen that causal direction without requiring a larger benchmark.

## Major critique 4: instrument failure must remain basis- and operator-specific

E6 is a useful experiment. The primary problem is its interpretation.

Full isolated K/V replacement changes native state at a formed-store cut. Transcoder-feature interchange changes reconstructed feature coefficients while keeping clean reconstruction-error state. Those are operator analogues in different representations, not identical state interventions. Holding unexplained error terms fixed is especially consequential when the model's identity dependence may sit there. Anthropic explicitly warns that error nodes can obscure mechanisms in [Circuit Tracing](https://www.transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html#reconstruction-errors-and-dark-matter).

The strongest claim is therefore: **the tested Gemma transcoder basis and its supported feature interventions do not expose this identity store's full causal control**. That is scientifically interesting and relevant to instrument builders. It does not show that all sparse dictionaries, all graph constructions, or QK tracing necessarily fail.

Other necessary refinements:

- Attribution at L0 is source ancestry/influence, not a claim that the store was committed at L0. Compare like questions.
- The draft's own multi-layer SAE result removes 63% of identity effect and 90% of color effect. Preserve this substantial positive tool result alongside the miss.
- ACDC discovers sets of edges; activation-patching studies use joint windows; circuit-tracer supports cumulative/multiple-feature interventions. Their expressive capabilities are not inherently singleton-only. [Conmy et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14997), [Zhang & Nanda](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.16042), and the [Circuit Tracing intervention methods](https://www.transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html#validating-attribution-graph-hypotheses-with-interventions) directly establish this.
- E19 rules out a narrow adjacent backup explanation on the specified panel. It does not rule out broader redundancy, non-contiguous coalitions, or adaptive downstream self-repair. Preserve those alternatives.

A compact feature-plus-error decomposition intervention would be a useful diagnostic for E6. It is not necessary to complete the present report and should not become a new broad certification suite.

## Major critique 5: the literature overlap is substantial, but it does not erase the contribution

The requested Sol-high reviewers agree that no inspected predecessor packages the complete diffusion–transformer–Mamba cut-aware comparison, isolated necessity/authorship, commit profiles, and read intervention. That is a meaningful synthesis contribution. It is a scoped literature-search conclusion, not proof of absolute priority.

The premise “if others had prior art, they would have published the cross-architecture findings” is not a reliable priority test. Several **did** publish cross-model or cross-family findings, while asking different questions and using different cuts. The fair question is which specific measurement or mechanism the present work adds.

The closest comparisons are:

- **Distributed transformer interventions:** [Cheng & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04061) report singleton failure and multi-position transfer across Llama, Qwen and Gemma. Their axis is positions at one layer, rather than this paper's formed-state depth trace.
- **Deferred commitment:** [Agarwal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17537) reports early entity availability and late final-token control across four decoder transformers. The added value here is isolated K/V necessity, exhaustive cuts, commit accounting/read replacement, and recurrent/diffusion comparison; late commitment alone is not new.
- **Diffusion timing:** [Prompt-to-Prompt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01626) explicitly varies intervention duration. [PCI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.08486) studies concept lock-in across five diffusion/flow models. [DifFRACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15796) builds timestep-conditioned FLUX graphs and tests early/late suppression. “They find where; we add when” is untenable. DifFRACT is especially important because the earlier FLUX paper already identified it as the closest related program, yet the consolidated draft omits it.
- **Causal CoT:** [Kudo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01113) already study iterative causal arithmetic CoT computation with large counterfactual token/layer grids across multiple model families. [Sequential Activation Patching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22332) explicitly targets temporally distributed reasoning effects. E10's distinctive addition is exact formed-K/V page graft/unmount/remount with visible text held fixed.
- **Static/dynamic editing and installation:** [Stable Flow](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Avrahami_Stable_Flow_Vital_Layers_for_Training-Free_Image_Editing_CVPR_2025_paper.html), [KV-Edit](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Zhu_KV-Edit_Training-Free_Image_Editing_for_Precise_Background_Preservation_ICCV_2025_paper.html), and [Padding Tone](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.389/) anticipate fixed routes, trajectory-indexed state injection, and text-stream registers. Distinguish the new measured decomposition from the broad engineering abstraction.

Related-work repair should make the paper more convincing, not diminish it: previous work supplied pieces; this work separates interventions that those pieces often conflated and compares their anatomy across execution substrates.

## Presentation, measurement, and reproducibility repairs

1. **Choose one abstract and one claim hierarchy.** Lead with the updated asymmetric result. Keep the complete four-quadrant diffusion example, mixed cells, and instrument-specific comparison distinct.
2. **Promote the most revealing figures.** The write/necessity scatter and per-layer profiles communicate the result more directly than the historical discovery timeline. A short discovery narrative is useful, but the timeline can move to an appendix.
3. **Regenerate figures after collation.** The bundled Figure 2 differs from a fresh render using the current figure source/reports. In particular, the existing image labels a point around (0.18, 0.74) as Gemma identity; current report/source place Gemma identity at **(0.07754, 0.80907)**, nearly overlapping Qwen identity, and Qwen color at **(0.17753, 0.74546)**. A fresh review-only render is saved as [figure-check](figure-check-2026-10-01_094028_PDT.png). The manuscript's figures were not overwritten.
4. **Explain Figure 5's incomparable grains.** A head, a complete layer's subject K/V, and a denoising-step route have different intervention sizes. The comparison is illustrative, not a calibrated architecture ranking. Contiguous windows are not globally optimal coalitions. Do not imply a proven minimal size from widths 2/3/4/6/8 alone.
5. **Give the metric equations in this paper.** Define pc, s, P, dp, normalized logprob gain, “necessity share,” floor, image class, and denominator eligibility. Readers should not need several drafts to understand a headline scalar. Separate candidate-set correctness from full-vocabulary correctness where relevant.
6. **State approximate replay accurately.** Transformer reports show numerical no-op errors of order 1e-4; some FLUX/VM results are byte-exact. “Bit-for-bit gate on every branch” should not stand in for a bounded numerical gate. The small errors do not plausibly explain order-one effects, but precise language matters.
7. **Separate architectural identities from learned findings.** Static SDXL cross-attention K/V follow from a fixed conditioner and fixed projections. A complete Möbius decomposition of every factorial cell reconstructs by algebra. Exact installation of all sufficient conditioning state is a useful runtime parity result. Learned semantic factorization, compact prediction, unseen composition, and mechanism discovery need separate evidence.
8. **Clarify “coordinate-free.”** Stable route roles plus low selected-coordinate overlap support robustness to the tested coordinate/position changes; they do not establish arbitrary basis invariance. Per-module scalar profile cosine is not necessarily shared tensor direction.
9. **Report admission and uncertainty without discarding trends.** Tens of admitted items are enough for a focused causal case study. Show their parent denominators and repeated-template structure. The E19 bootstrap is row-wise, and best-window selection is exploratory; confidence intervals do not automatically certify a population-optimal window. Preserve all heterogeneity and countertrends.
10. **Publish the current core, not just superseded certificates.** Provide pinned code, prompt panels, selected raw/per-row results and compact offline verification for E17/E19, Mamba, E6 and E10. Preserve the old receipts as historical records with an obvious superseded status. Private demo citations and TODO R6 are release-preparation gaps, not evidence that their effects are absent.
11. **Label the six FLUX row scores.** Values such as fur 0.894 are `rank_score`, not the ≥0.90 sufficiency gate observable. The ledger's certified weaker-seed sufficiency values are ≥0.90. Put the actual P values in a labeled column or explain the composite score; otherwise the printed gate and rows appear contradictory.

## Grading fairly against peers

The author's point about evidence and coverage is justified. Many respected circuit papers investigate one model and one behavior, or demonstrate a method with a small set of detailed examples. A mechanistic paper does not need to outperform a product benchmark to be publishable. Cross-substrate breadth and deeper interventions are legitimate strengths, even when the natural-task sample count is small.

The converse also matters: some nearest temporal-diffusion and CoT peers use substantially larger panels, so it would be inaccurate to say this paper dominates them on every evidence axis. Breadth, mechanism depth, natural-task coverage, and reproduction are different dimensions.

| Dimension | Grade | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Causal intervention depth | A- | Richer operator/cut coverage than many one-method case studies; strong native-output and restore discipline. |
| Execution-substrate breadth | A- | DiT/diffusion, decoder transformers, and three Mamba sizes are unusual breadth. Different outcomes are a strength for the framework. |
| Distinctive mechanistic contribution | B+/A- | Necessity/authorship asymmetry and commit/read anatomy remain strong after prior-art correction. The unique-class theorem does not. |
| Natural-behavior and population coverage | B- | Selected identity/color/arithmetic panels and small ancillary examples limit universal statements. This does not invalidate discovery. |
| Evidence transparency | B+ internally; B-/C+ for an external reader | Clear receipts/retractions and harness are good; current core still depends heavily on private records. |
| Prior-art positioning and claim precision | C+ | Missing close temporal/CoT papers and contradictory headline statements materially affect review. |
| Submission readiness | B- | Major revision needed; the main fixes are scope, organization, figures, and public evidence packaging. |

Relative to a focused interpretability workshop paper, this is an unusually substantial package. Relative to a strong main-conference mechanism paper, it is competitive in causal depth and architecture breadth, but presently weaker in narrative discipline and independently usable artifacts. No guarantee of venue acceptance follows from those comparisons.

## Does it address unanswered questions and enable CoT tracking?

It answers several **bounded** questions well:

- Can an internal path show a large joint effect while each tested singleton is dispensable for the chosen outcome? Yes, in the FLUX case and on the necessity side of selected LM cells.
- Does a necessary formed-state region have to be the unique place where an edit works? No; the residual-write and isolated-state profiles separate those questions.
- Does a superficially distributed certificate survive exhaustive sweeps? Often no; the retractions are an important methodological result.
- Does one sparse explanatory basis expose all causally relevant state? E6 gives a concrete negative answer for the tested identity store.
- Can a written, non-restated arithmetic intermediate's cache state influence a later answer with visible text fixed? Yes, on E10's admitted items.
- Can semantic attention addressing be recovered from the query alone? The current spectral method does not do so on the semantic panels; the negative result is informative.

Some of these questions have prior partial answers. The new value lies in sharper separation and the combined causal record, rather than claiming that every question was wholly unanswered.

For CoT, distinguish three levels:

1. **Demonstrated:** reversible manipulation of a step's formed state, followed by native continuation, can test causal dependence and directed counterfactual use.
2. **Plausible application:** a causal debugger could connect intermediate states to later decisions and distinguish used, rewritten, redundant, or ignored steps. The current VM supplies useful mechanics for that investigation.
3. **Not demonstrated:** an automated monitor of freely generated long reasoning, hidden motives, deceptive reasoning, or full latent computation in frontier models. Causal influence of a written intermediate is not complete reconstruction of the reasoning process, and offline causal interventions on already-generated scratchpad state do not establish prospective monitoring performance. E10's interventions occur before the final answer is generated.

This distinction is relevant to current frontier-lab priorities. OpenAI explicitly studies [CoT monitorability](https://openai.com/index/evaluating-chain-of-thought-monitorability/) and [controllability](https://openai.com/index/reasoning-models-chain-of-thought-controllability/), while Anthropic studies [unfaithful reasoning traces](https://www.anthropic.com/research/reasoning-models-dont-say-think) and [mechanistic CoT faithfulness](https://www.transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html#chain-of-thought-faithfulness). An internal causal assay can complement these programs. Their published interest establishes topic relevance; it does not establish their reaction to this manuscript.

The smallest persuasive next CoT demonstration is not another huge arithmetic grid. Use a few freely generated reasoning traces with a meaningful ambiguity or error. Predict which consumed state will alter a later decision, intervene at that state with text fixed, verify the predicted directed change, and include an irrelevant-step control and exact restore. Compare what the state assay reveals with a text-only monitor. This would demonstrate added diagnostic value beyond already-known causal use of arithmetic CoT. Frontier-scale coverage would be needed for a frontier-general claim, not for initial scientific attention.

## Recommended submission story and minimum revision

An accurate headline would be:

> Single-site localization and whole-path state interventions answer different questions. Across diffusion, transformer, and recurrent models, exact causal tests reveal distributed conditioning, late formed-state commitment, and concentrated or redundant writers; a sparse feature basis can miss dependence visible at the native state cut.

Possible titles include **“Space and Time Circuits: Cut-Specific Causal Anatomy Across Neural Architectures”** or **“Distributed Stores and Late Commitment Across Neural Architectures.”** The existing title can remain if the operational nature and mixed cases are explicit.

Before submission, do these in order:

1. Reconcile every headline and same-model label with E15–E19 and E10; remove the false linear-discrimination assertion.
2. Separate diffusion's four-quadrant result, transformer necessity/write asymmetry, and Mamba's concentrated retention writer. Make their differences central.
3. Repair the related-work comparisons, especially deferred commitment, DifFRACT/PCI, and causal/sequential CoT interventions.
4. Make E6 a fair basis-specific comparison and the ledger approximate/descriptive where appropriate.
5. Choose one abstract, define metrics locally, regenerate figures, and publish a small current-evidence bundle.

Those revisions can make the paper credible without more large runs. Small discriminating clock, joint-mask, or free-CoT examples would further strengthen specific claims. They should be chosen for the unresolved mechanism, not imposed as generic publication gates.

**My recommendation:** publish the strong bounded story and make it easy to reproduce. The paper has enough depth and scope to attract serious attention. Its best route to that attention is precision about what the new evidence establishes.
