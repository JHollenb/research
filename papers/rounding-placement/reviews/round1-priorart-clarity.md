## 3. Title + abstract
Alt titles: (A) **"Where You Round Beats How Many Bits: One Mechanism for Four Low-Precision Transformer Failures."** (B) **"Remove the Invariant First: Coordinate-Frame Rounding in Low-Precision Transformers."**
≤200-word abstract (leads with the most actionable claim):
> Before any low-precision cast of transformer keys, subtract the per-head key mean — one vector per head. We show this single operation takes a full BF16 training step's attention-gradient error from 204% to 11.8% on Pythia-160m, the dominant fix for the late-training FlashAttention blowup, and that it is the same operation that repairs 4-bit KV caches and streaming RoPE drift at inference. The reason is one mechanism: a transformer's exact symmetries (a softmax shift, a per-channel scale, an absolute-position shift) hide a large invariant inside each stored value; rounding in the model's native coordinates spends the mantissa on the invariant and erases the content the model actually uses. Error is therefore set by the coordinate frame, not the bit count — a 16-bit log-polar format is 5× worse than BF16, and FP32 position angles collapse at exactly 2^24 regardless of value bits. One recipe fixes all four failures: remove the invariant in high precision, carry phase as an integer, restore the conservation law after rounding. Measured on six Qwen/Pythia checkpoints; no end-to-end training benefit shown yet.
