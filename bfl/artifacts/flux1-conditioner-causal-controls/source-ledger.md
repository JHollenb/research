# BFL failure replay: closing the loop with Saturn

This is the failure-first companion to [the current BFL top-20 ranking](2026-08-15-bfl-new-top-20-saturn-upgrades.md).
The previous ranking correctly preserved several strong results, but it mixed three different
things under the word “miss”: a failed terminal gate, an exploratory branch that was weaker than
its control, and an experiment whose claim was deliberately narrower than the question we wanted
to ask. This report separates them and replays the actionable misses through Saturn.

The original reports and images are preserved unchanged. Every replay below gets a new receipt
and a new artifact directory. A rerun can upgrade the interpretation, but it cannot rewrite the
original result. “Preserved” here means append-only/custodied in the workspace; it does not imply
that every object store has retention locks or versioning enabled.

## Operating rule

For each miss we follow the typed causal chain:

`producer → typed write → carrier trajectory → native consumer → rendered behavior`.

The first replay is the smallest informative one: exact checkpoint resume, native duplicate,
candidate intervention, zero/no-op, and the relevant wrong-site, wrong-time, wrong-source, dose,
sign, or norm-matched-sham control. Only after locating the failing boundary do we change the
intervention or fit a replacement. Saturn’s controller must record the candidate, promotion or
rejection, exact rollback, and downstream consumer score in the same resident lease.

Evidence labels follow the workspace policy:

- **observation** — directly measured or visibly rendered;
- **trend** — directional but not yet robust;
- **convergent trend** — supported by independent seeds, tools, or contexts;
- **working inference** — current explanation plus alternatives;
- **terminal claim** — bounded conclusion that earned its gates.

## Independent audit

Luna’s read-only audit agrees with the failure boundary above: the next semantic linker should
retain the additive linear state and add a consumer-closed late span/multi-site residual. A new
scalar feature basis is not the right repair. The audit also corrected the scope of the ledger:
the full-donor function-recovery result is recoverability evidence, not a failed compiler; the
carrier atlas, promptless route, context scaffold, sub-act panel, head sensitivity, clock, exact
replay, and VAE are bounded instruments or engineering controls rather than failed semantic
ownership claims. The current held-out circuit artifact has `axis_count: 6`, so the older top-20
denominator (`5 strict, 14 carrier, 1 candidate`) is a reporting mismatch, not scientific
evidence.

Terra’s independent read-only QA then checked the replay receipts, configs, worker hashes, and
artifact custody. Terra confirmed the snake, temporal, seam, structured, Hotpatch, dense, and
certificate panels are control-complete within their stated scopes, but required four language
changes applied below: action is fixed-specimen route reconstruction rather than pose ownership;
scene remains a collateral/disentanglement question; FLUX.1 is not evidently dominated by wiring
but still lacks its causal control panel; and one rejected mixer proposal cannot prove basis
undercapacity. Terra’s highest-priority additions—fresh action/scene controls and the FLUX.1
panel—are now replayed and archived.

Pascal’s pre-run Luna audit checked the consumer-closed semantic-operation replay itself. It found
the typed return hooks, affine/low-rank/route branches, and checkpoint no-op accounting coherent,
but flagged the endpoint as RGB-only: without wrong-source/random semantic controls, an RGB gain
can still be a color or texture proxy. The resulting FLUX.2 replay is therefore reported as an
exploratory frozen-suffix operation test, not as semantic recovery or a certified compiler.

Fresh Terra QA of the semantic-bridge closure byte-verified all 30 persisted PNGs and all 22
remote branch fields against the aggregate report. It confirmed the one-lease identity, exact
checkpoint no-ops, full-donor rescue, full text+image ceiling, and wrong-prompt specificity, but
also noted that the sham is only a recipient-state no-op, the central-support/background values
are spatial proxies rather than subject masks, and the receipt has no retention-enforced object
lock, worker/source hash, or full-weight digest. The closure is therefore durable and
self-consistent, not a terminal custody claim.

Fresh Luna review independently confirmed the arithmetic, visual direction, exact full
text+image ceiling, and restricted-branch failures. It also caught the seed distinction between
the diffusion replay identity and the family checkpoint initialization, and rejected stronger
wording such as “smallest donor state,” “necessary coalition,” or “semantic bridge.” Those
claims remain explicitly open below.

## Artifact policy

Canonical replays aim to retain the mrun receipt, exact request/config hash, worker hash,
checkpoint identity, native duplicate, controls, images, and a short analysis. Legacy or worker
specific gaps are recorded as custody limitations rather than silently filled in; for example,
the semantic-bridge closure has a null guarded receipt and no worker/source or full-weight digest.
Failed hardware admission or output-custody jobs are execution diagnostics and are never counted
as scientific misses. A missing gate is “not established by this test,” not evidence that the
mechanism is absent.
