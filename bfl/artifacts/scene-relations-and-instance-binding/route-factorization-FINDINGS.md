# Target direction works when the shared native action state is present

The follow-up separates an inactive compact action from target-direction failure. A full `joint.3` text replacement using the captured left/right native source produces requested-side camera contact in 6/6 seed × target images. Holding the predicate slots at a shared mean while changing only the sign of the complementary text-state difference also produces requested-side contact in 6/6 images. Spectral exact-instance reads select that sign. This is a consumer-connected donor-assisted target route at bounded scope.

Changing only the three predicate slots around the same shared mean selects the requested side in 5/6 cases: seed 26091741 still touches the left camera under the right request. The shared mean alone touches left in 3/3 seeds. Thus neither averaging nor the absence of a signed target term makes the image semantically neutral. Directional influence is distributed, and the full contextual state matters.

## Mathematical cut

Let H_L and H_R be the contextual native states at a particular step, M=(H_L+H_R)/2, D=(H_R-H_L)/2, and P the diagonal projector onto the three predicate rows. The projected branches replace the full text state by M + beta(I-P)D or M + beta P D, with beta selected by the spectral camera instance. In the complement pair, PH'_L=PM=PH'_R: the predicate values are fixed while the camera receiving contact changes. This is stronger target-route evidence than merely reading distinct object IDs from a registry.

However, M and D were both captured from the target-conditioned native futures of the recipient scene. They are not independently learned geometry or a reusable donor-free object pointer. The complement contains 92.11–94.93% of the directional energy; predicate-only versus complement is not an equal-energy comparison and does not establish that one contains more causal information per unit norm. All target-side outcomes are coarse contact observations by one unblinded model reviewer.
